If you have found this page you are probably somewhere specific: growth has flattened, the marketing that used to work has stopped working, and hiring a full-time marketing director feels like a bigger commitment than the problem currently justifies.

That is exactly the gap the fractional model was built for. It is a real, sensible answer. It is also, quite often, the wrong shape of answer, and it is worth knowing which before you sign anything.

## What the job actually is

A fractional CMO is an experienced marketing leader who works on your leadership team part time. Typically:

- **One to three days a week**, though some work in blocks rather than fixed days.
- **A rolling contract**, usually with a notice period measured in months rather than weeks.
- **Somebody who has done the job full time**, usually at a larger business than yours.

They are not a freelancer who does the marketing. They are the person who decides what the marketing should be, sets the standards, and answers for the number. If there is nobody to direct, there is not much for them to do.

## When it is genuinely the right hire

Three things have to be true, and they have to be true together.

**You already know the constraint is marketing.** Not "growth has slowed and marketing is the bit we can see", but you have looked, and demand really is the thing holding the business back. That distinction is the whole article.

**There are people to lead.** A fractional CMO working with no team becomes an expensive strategist producing plans nobody executes. The model works when the gap is direction rather than hands.

**The work is continuous.** Marketing for you is an operating function that needs running week after week, not a project with an end. If it has an end, you want a consultant on a defined piece of work, and it will cost you less.

## The failure mode nobody mentions

Here is what goes wrong most often, and it has nothing to do with the quality of the person you hire.

A business decides it needs marketing leadership because demand is the symptom it can see. Underneath, three things are true at once: enquiries are arriving and not converting because the handover between marketing and sales is broken, margin is thin because pricing has not been revisited in three years, and the team is doing by hand what a system should be doing.

A good fractional CMO will diagnose all three correctly. Then they will be able to act on roughly one of them, because the other two sit in somebody else's remit. The honest ones say so in month one.

At that point you are either living with a third of the answer, or you are hiring a fractional COO as well, and paying two part-time leaders to negotiate the boundary between them.

**The underlying problem is that a seat is not a diagnosis.** Deciding you need a CMO is deciding what the answer is before anybody has established what the question was.

## What to ask before you hire one

Ask these of any fractional CMO you are considering. Ask them of anybody else you are considering too, including us.

1. **What would you look at first, and why that?** You are testing whether they start with your business or with their playbook.
2. **What would make you say this is not a marketing problem?** If they cannot answer this, they have never said it, and you do not want to be the first client they say it to.
3. **What does success look like, as a number, and when would we know?** Vagueness here is the most expensive kind.
4. **What happens to this when you are not here any more?** A function that collapses on departure was never built.
5. **What would make you tell me to stop paying you?** The best answer to this question is specific and slightly uncomfortable.

## The short version

A fractional CMO is a good answer to a marketing problem in a business with a marketing team and a continuous need. It is an expensive answer to a business problem that happens to be showing up in the marketing numbers.

Work out which one you have first. That is a cheaper piece of work than either hire, and it is usually a conversation rather than a project.
