# 20 Summer Pub Events That Actually Make Money (Not Just Noise)

Most summer events fail on the same three things: they cost too much to set up, they need your best people all day, and they die the first time it rains. A packed garden that leaves you no better off is not a win.

The fix is to stop chasing spectaculars and build two or three formats you can run every week with the team you already have. Repeatable beats impressive, because habit is what turns a good Saturday into a good August.

I run The Anchor in Stanwell Moor, and the twenty formats below are the ones I would actually put on a whiteboard, with the sums you need before you commit to any of them.

For events beyond summer, see our full guide to [pub event ideas](/guides/pub-event-ideas) across every season and type.

## The maths of a summer event

Before you book anything, work out what the night has to earn.

Say you run a BBQ Saturday and it needs one extra chef and two extra bar staff for six hours. At a true £15 an hour, including employer's National Insurance and holiday pay, that is £270 of additional labour. Add £60 of charcoal, disposables and decoration. Your event has to clear £330 of gross profit before it has made you a penny.

Now the sell side. A burger, chips and a pint bundle at £14.95 is £12.46 excluding VAT. If the food costs you £2.40 and the pint costs you £1.30, your gross profit is £8.76 a head, about 70%. That is in line with the 65% to 70% most pubs target on food. Divide £330 by £8.76 and you need 38 bundles to break even, and every one after that is profit.

Thirty eight is a target you can put on a whiteboard and a target the team can beat. "Have a good one" is not. These are worked examples: run them on your own invoices and your own wage rates.

## Category 1: Garden parties and socials

- **Sunset spritz hour**, 5pm to 7pm every Friday, one drink at one price, no other decisions. Fixed windows train habit better than all-day offers.
- **Weekly garden party with a fixed drinks bundle**, for example a sharing jug plus four glasses at a set price for groups of four.
- **Acoustic Sunday sessions**, one performer, two sets, 4pm to 6pm so you are not paying for a late finish.
- **Garden games league.** Boules, giant Jenga, cornhole. Buy once, run all summer, no ongoing cost.
- **Silent disco** if noise is your constraint. Headphone hire is cheap and it removes the neighbour problem entirely.

The mistake to avoid: pricing a bundle you have not costed. Work out the gross profit on the jug before you print the board, not after.

## Category 2: Food-led events

Food is where the margin lives, because you control the plate cost and people stay longer.

- **BBQ nights with pre-booked tables** and a two or three item menu. Two items done fast beats six done slowly at 40 degrees in a marquee.
- **[Street food pop-ups](/guides/pop-up-events-for-pubs) with a partner.** Either charge a pitch fee or take a share of their takings, and keep every drop of the wet trade. Agree in writing who is responsible for their food hygiene registration.
- **Sharing board nights** for groups, priced per board rather than per head so parties self-select upwards.
- **Low and slow smoker day**, one cut of meat, one price, sold until it is gone. Scarcity does the marketing.
- **Ice cream window on Sunday afternoons** to catch families walking past who would never come inside.

Keep hot food above the temperatures your food safety management system specifies and probe it, especially chicken and burgers cooked outside. Have a written allergen list for every item on the BBQ, the same as you would indoors.

## Category 3: Sport and competitions

- **Match screenings with table deposits.** A £5 per head deposit redeemable against the bill kills the no-show problem on big fixtures.
- **Summer darts or pool knockout** run over six weeks, £5 entry, winner takes the pot, you take the bar.
- **Outdoor quiz** on a long summer evening, shorter than your winter format, four rounds and done by nine so the garden turns over.
- **Boules or petanque league** if you have a strip of garden and gravel. Cheap to build, runs itself once teams form.
- **Beat the landlord** challenges, which cost you nothing but a bit of dignity.

Team formats matter more than one-off nights. A six week league means six guaranteed busy evenings, not one.

## Category 4: Family and community days

- **Kids craft mornings** tied to a food booking, 10am to noon, so parents buy coffee and brunch.
- **Local market stalls** in the car park on a Saturday morning: stallholders pay a pitch fee and bring their own customers.
- **Village dog show.** Half a dozen daft classes, £2 entry per class to a local charity, rosettes off the internet. It is the cheapest way I know to fill a garden.
- **Classic car or bike meet** on a fixed Sunday each month.
- **Charity BBQs** with a named local cause, which unlocks shares from every group that supports it.

If you are hiring an inflatable, use an operator whose unit is inspected under a recognised scheme such as PIPA or ADIPS, made to BS EN 14960, and properly anchored. No inflatable should be used in winds above 24mph, so agree in advance who makes the call and get their public liability certificate on file before the day.

## Licence, weather and safety

**Licence.** Check the plan attached to your premises licence and confirm the garden sits inside the red line. If it does not, drinks carried out are off-sales and need the right permission. For music, the Live Music Act means live music between 8am and 11pm needs no extra permission at licensed premises open for on-sales, with amplified performances limited to audiences of 500. Go past 11pm, or licence a field that is not on your plan, and you need a Temporary Event Notice. A premises can use 15 TENs a year across no more than 21 days, capped at 499 people, and a standard TEN needs ten working days' notice, so diarise it early. Recorded music still needs TheMusicLicence, and our guide to [PRS and PPL music licensing](/guides/prs-ppl-music-licensing-pubs) explains what you owe and why.

**Weather.** Set a decision point, for example 5pm on the Thursday, and publish it in advance: "on rain or shine, inside if wet." Book gazebos rather than a marquee where you can, and never put non-refundable money into a single outdoor-only date.

**Safety.** Polycarbonate glassware outside, a marked route for hot food away from children, cables taped or covered, and a bin plan. Broken glass in a garden ends an event faster than rain.

## How to choose the right summer event

Run every idea through four filters:

1. Does it work in bad weather, or can it move indoors in twenty minutes?
2. Can the team deliver it weekly without your best chef being on shift?
3. Does it create a clear spend per head you can predict?
4. Would someone tell a friend about it in one sentence?

If it fails two of the four, park it. You do not have the labour to be adventurous every weekend.

## Make every summer event bookable

A great summer event is only half the job. Package each one as a named, bookable moment, put it on your Google listing and socials with a direct booking link, and use the night itself to capture details and invite people back.

Practically, that means three things. Give the event a name people can search, not "live music Saturday". Take bookings with a deposit or a card guarantee for anything over six people. And put a QR code on the table that leads to your mailing list with a reason to sign up, so August's crowd is still reachable in November.

That is how a single event turns into repeat trade rather than a one-off. For the full system around your events, see our [summer pub marketing playbook](/guides/summer-pub-marketing).

## Common mistakes

- **One-off big events with no follow-up.** The cost is front loaded and the benefit disappears the next morning.
- **Overcomplicated menus in peak heat.** Long tickets in a hot kitchen mean slow service and bad reviews.
- **No plan for rain.** Decide the rule in advance and tell people what it is.
- **Under-staffing the bar to protect the wage line.** A twenty minute queue in a garden costs more in unserved rounds than the shift you cut.
- **Free entry with nothing attached.** Every event should have a bundle, a deposit or a minimum spend behind it.

## Quick checklist

- Two summer formats chosen and costed to a break-even number of covers.
- Booking system live with deposits on larger tables.
- Rain plan written and published.
- Licence plan checked, TENs applied for where needed.
- Weekly promotion schedule set for the whole season.
- Data capture running at every event.

## Mini FAQ

**How often should I run summer events?**
Weekly is best if the format is simple. Consistency builds habit, and habit is cheaper than advertising.

**Do I need to charge entry?**
Not always. Deposits or bundles usually work better because they lift spend without putting a barrier on the gate.

**What should I do if only twelve people turn up?**
Run it anyway, run it well, and keep the date. Give a format a couple of months to build, because cancelling teaches people not to trust your diary.
