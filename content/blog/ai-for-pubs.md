# AI for Pubs: What Is New, What Works and Where to Start

This guide is for licensees who have tried ChatGPT once or twice and want to know what is worth doing with it now. It is not another getting-started piece. It assumes you can open an assistant and type a question, and goes from there.

It covers what has changed for pubs, how to set an assistant up once, twelve jobs worth handing over with prompts you can copy, what the slash, at and hash signs really do, and the house rules that keep you out of trouble. It does not recommend paid software, quote prices or forecast what AI will do next.

Start with a job, not a tool. Pick something that eats your week, such as the posts, the specials board or the replies to party enquiries, and give that job to the assistant you already have. Shopping for software comes later, if at all.

AI makes your own ideas happen faster, but it does not supply them. The section near the end shows how we use it at The Anchor, our own venue.

The details about each tool in this guide were checked on 14 October 2026. AI products change often, so a menu or a setting may have moved by the time you read this.

## What has changed

**Guests now use AI to choose where to go.** 19% of British consumers already use AI tools, such as ChatGPT or search engine summaries, to plan a visit to a pub, bar or restaurant, and half are considering it. The figure comes from the [GO Technology report by Zonal and NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/insights/report/2026/nearly-a-fifth-of-consumers-use-ai-tools-to-plan-a-visit-to-a-pub-bar-or-restaurant/), published in October 2026, a survey of more than 5,000 British consumers. Of those who do, 45% use it to look for reviews and ratings, 43% to check typical prices, 38% to view menus and 35% to look up opening times.

**Search now links straight to a booking.** Since April 2026, [Google's AI Mode in the UK](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/around-the-globe/google-europe/united-kingdom/ai-mode-restaurants-uk/) has helped people find somewhere to eat and linked them straight to booking partners, including TheFork, SevenRooms, ResDiary, Mozrest, Foodhub, Dojo, DesignMyNight and OpenTable.

**Businesses are catching up.** Three in ten UK businesses (30%) reported using at least one AI technology in September 2026, up from 9% in September 2023, according to the [Office for National Statistics](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/businessservices/bulletins/businessinsightsandimpactontheukeconomy/8october2026). In accommodation and food services the figure was about one in five in June 2026, and the [Office for National Statistics found](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/businessservices/articles/artificialintelligenceinukbusinesses/2023to2026) that businesses in accommodation and food services most commonly use free AI software.

**Not every guest likes it.** In August 2026 the [BBC reported](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cq89jpjeq7ko) a backlash against AI-made posters and menus in bars and restaurants in Northern Ireland: one Belfast bar declared itself an AI-free zone, and a restaurant owner found "Say no to AI" stickers on his flyers. The house rules below cover what that means for your own pictures.

**The tools can now take a longer job.** The paid versions of the main assistants can now be given a job with several steps and left to work through it. OpenAI calls its version ChatGPT Work and launched it for paid plans in July 2026, according to its [release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes). That is the maker's description, not a test of ours.

**Features come and go.** OpenAI has announced that custom GPTs will be retired on 11 December 2026. Build your habits around the basics, which last, rather than around the newest button.

What guests read about you matters more than which tool you pick, so that is where to begin.

## See your pub the way AI does

The quickest useful thing you can do with AI is ask it about yourself. Open the assistant you already use, make sure it can search the web (in ChatGPT, type `/` and choose Search), and ask the questions a guest would ask. Replace the words in capitals with your own.

> Where would you recommend for Sunday lunch near [TOWN]? Give me three places and say why.
>
> What are the opening hours and food service times at [PUB NAME] in [TOWN]? Tell me where you found them.
>
> I am booking a birthday meal for twelve near [TOWN]. Which pubs take group bookings?

Read the answers as a guest would. Are you in them? Are the hours right? Does it know that you serve food on a Sunday, take group bookings or have a function room? Write down anything that is wrong or missing.

Then fix the source, not the answer. Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in its AI answers, and no special technical work. [Its own checklist](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) includes having important content available as text and keeping Business Profile information up to date. For a pub, that means:

- your menu, opening hours and food service times written as text on your own website, not only in a photograph or a PDF ([does your pub need a website?](/guides/does-your-pub-need-a-website));
- your [Google Business Profile](/guides/google-business-profile-pub-guide) checked against your real hours and up to date;
- the same details wherever else you are listed, so nothing contradicts them.

Ask the same three questions again a month later and see what has changed.

## Set it up once

Use the assistant you already have. If the team already uses ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot or Claude, set that one up properly rather than adding another. The steps below are for ChatGPT; the house rules section gives the matching privacy switch for the others.

**First, switch off training.** In ChatGPT, go to Settings, then Data controls, then switch off "Improve the model for everyone". That stops your chats being used to train the model. Do it before you type anything about the business.

**Then tell it who you are, once.** ChatGPT keeps standing instructions, called custom instructions, that apply to every chat. On the web they are under Settings, then Personalization. On a phone they are under Settings, then Customize ChatGPT. Free accounts get 1,500 characters, which is plenty. Copy this, replace the words in capitals and cut anything that does not apply:

> I run [PUB NAME], a [village local / town-centre pub / food-led pub] in [TOWN]. We serve [FOOD STYLE] and open [DAYS AND HOURS]. Our regulars are [WHO THEY ARE]. This year we want more [MIDWEEK DINERS / PRIVATE HIRE / SUNDAY BOOKINGS].
>
> Write in British English, in a warm, plain voice, the way a good landlord talks. Short sentences. No hashtags unless I ask for them.
>
> Never invent prices, dates, opening times, awards or allergen information. If you need one, ask me.
>
> If I type /short, answer in three sentences or fewer.

The last line is how you make a shortcut word of your own: the assistant knows nothing about /short until your instructions tell it what the word means.

**Keep each job in one place.** "New project" in the sidebar keeps the chats, files and instructions for one job together. Free accounts can add five files to a project. One project for events, with your events list in it, means you never paste the same details twice. A second project for supplier paperwork keeps those chats out of the way.

**Use a temporary chat for anything you would rather not keep.** Selecting Temporary when you start a new chat keeps that conversation out of your history, and it is not used for training.

## Twelve jobs worth handing over

Each prompt below is ready to copy. Replace the words in square brackets, and take out names and contact details before you paste anything in. For the jobs that start with a photograph, tap the + icon and choose "Add photos & files"; free accounts are limited to three file uploads a day. You can also tap the microphone icon to dictate instead of typing, or the Voice icon to talk it through. Both are on all plans, with limits on free accounts.

The last of the twelve is a scheduled task. ChatGPT can run a task on a schedule, managed under "Scheduled" in the sidebar. Free accounts can have three active tasks, each running at most once a day.

Every prompt ends with a check, because these tools can state wrong things as fact. For more on posts, see the [social media guide](/guides/social-media-strategy-for-pubs).

### One event, three posts

> Here are the details of an event at my pub, between the ### marks. Write three versions: a Facebook post of under 80 words, an Instagram caption of under 50 words, and a two-line update for my Google Business Profile. Use only the facts I have given.
>
> \###
> [EVENT, DATE, TIME, PRICE, HOW TO BOOK]
> \###

**Check before you use it:** the date, the time and the price.

### A reply to a review

> Between the triple quotation marks is a review a customer left for my pub. Draft a reply of under 90 words. Thank them, answer the point they raised, and do not offer anything I have not mentioned.
>
> """
> [PASTE THE REVIEW, WITHOUT THE REVIEWER'S NAME]
> """

**Check before you use it:** that every fact in the reply is true. Send it as yourself.

### A reply to a party enquiry

> Draft a reply to an enquiry about hiring our function room. What they asked is between the ### marks. Ask for the date, the numbers, the timings and the budget if any are missing. Ask whether anyone in the party has a food allergy, intolerance or coeliac disease. Do not quote a price.
>
> \###
> [WHAT THEY ASKED, WITHOUT THEIR NAME OR CONTACT DETAILS]
> \###

**Check before you use it:** that it promises nothing you cannot deliver.

### The specials board, typed up

> This is a photograph of today's specials board. Type it up as a list, then write a post of under 60 words about the two dishes you would lead with. Do not describe any dish as free from an allergen.

**Check before you use it:** dish names and prices against the board.

### Order against delivery

> These two photographs are my order and the delivery note. List anything that is missing, short or substituted.

**Check before you use it:** the list against the goods in front of you, and any substitution against your allergen chart.

### Two price lists, one table

> These are price lists from two suppliers. Build a table of the items that appear on both, with each price and the difference. List separately anything you could not match.

**Check before you use it:** pack sizes and units, then the arithmetic.

### Menu descriptions

> Rewrite these dish descriptions so that each is under 20 words and says what the guest will taste. Keep every ingredient I have listed and add none. Do not mention allergens or make any dietary claim.
>
> \###
> [DISH NAMES AND INGREDIENTS]
> \###

**Check before you use it:** that each description matches the dish you serve.

### A run sheet for an event

> Write a run sheet for a [QUIZ NIGHT / LIVE MUSIC NIGHT / PRIVATE PARTY] at my pub, from two hours before doors to close. Give each step a time, the job to do and the role responsible. Use roles, not names.

**Check before you use it:** timings, and your licence conditions.

### Tonight's briefing

> Turn these notes into a team briefing of under 120 words that I can read out before service. Use roles, not names.
>
> \###
> [NUMBER OF BOOKINGS AND TIMES, SPECIALS, WHAT IS OFF, WHAT TO RECOMMEND]
> \###

**Check before you use it:** that nothing in it names a guest.

### A letter in plain English

> Explain this letter in plain English. List what it asks me to do and by when, and the questions I should put to my accountant, my solicitor or my business development manager. Do not advise me on what to do.

**Check before you use it:** cover names and account numbers before you photograph it. It explains, it does not advise.

### The week's posts, every Monday

> Every Monday at 8am, using the events list in this project, draft this week's posts: one for today, one for Thursday and one for Saturday morning. Under 70 words each. Tell me which facts you were unsure of.

**Check before you use it:** everything. A scheduled draft is still a draft.

### Thinking it through out loud

> I am going to talk through a problem at my pub. Ask me one question at a time until you understand it, then give me three options and the drawback of each.

**Check before you use it:** the decision is yours.

## The shortcuts worth knowing

Much of what is shared online about AI shortcuts is wrong. Here is what the three signs really do.

**The slash.** When you are signed in to ChatGPT, typing `/` in the message box opens a menu of tools. Choosing Search makes ChatGPT look things up on the live web. Typing `/Deepresearch` starts a longer piece of research with sources.

**The at sign.** In Gemini, typing `@` and choosing an app, such as Google Calendar, tells it which of your connected apps to use. In ChatGPT, `@` picks an app or plugin you have added.

**The hash.** The `#` sign is not a command in ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini or Claude. It marks a heading, and OpenAI's guidance says headings help mark the separate sections of a long request.

Where three hash signs earn their place is in fencing off text. [OpenAI's own guidance](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6654000-best-practices-for-prompt-engineering-with-the-openai-api) is to put the instruction first and to separate it from pasted text with `###` or with triple quotation marks. That way the assistant cannot mistake your notes for your instructions:

> Rewrite the notes between the ### marks as a Facebook post of under 80 words. Keep every fact and add none.
>
> \###
> [PASTE YOUR NOTES HERE]
> \###

Everything worth knowing, in one table:

| What you type or tap | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | Opens a menu of tools. Choose Search to make it check the live web | ChatGPT, signed in |
| `/Deepresearch` | Starts a longer piece of research with sources | ChatGPT, signed in |
| `@` | Picks an app you have connected, such as Google Calendar | Gemini. In ChatGPT it picks an app or plugin you have added |
| `###` or triple quotation marks | Fences off text you paste in from your instruction | Any assistant |
| `#` at the start of a line | Marks a heading in a long request. It is not a command | Any assistant |
| The + icon, then "Add photos & files" | Shows it a photograph or a document | ChatGPT |
| The microphone icon | Lets you dictate instead of typing | ChatGPT |
| Ctrl+K, or Cmd+K on a Mac | Searches your past chats | ChatGPT on the web |
| "Temporary", when you start a new chat | Keeps that conversation out of your history | ChatGPT |
| "New project", in the sidebar | Keeps one job's chats, files and instructions together | ChatGPT |
| The Copilot key, or the Windows key and C | Opens Copilot | Windows PCs |

**The "secret codes".** The slash "codes" passed around online, such as /ELI5 or /TLDR, are not features of any assistant. They work only as ordinary words the assistant reads, so results vary. If you want a shortcut word of your own, define it in your custom instructions, as the /short line in the set-up section does.

## House rules: what never goes into AI

Agree these before anyone uses AI for the pub. Each one comes with its reason.

1. **Guests' details, staff records and allergy information stay out of free and personal AI accounts.** Data protection law applies in full to AI tools: the [Information Commissioner's Office says](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/our-work-on-artificial-intelligence/response-to-the-consultation-series-on-generative-ai/tackling-misconceptions/) there is no AI exemption. A guest's allergy is information about their health, which data protection law protects more strictly. If you want help with a reply, take the name and details out first. The guide to [food allergies and data protection](/guides/food-allergies-gdpr-compliance) covers the rest.
2. **A named person checks every date, price, opening time and claim before anything an AI tool wrote is used.** The [National Cyber Security Centre warns](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/ai-and-cyber-security-what-you-need-to-know) that these tools can get things wrong and present incorrect statements as facts.
3. **AI never decides what is in a dish.** The food business is legally responsible for the accuracy of its allergen information, however that information is produced ([Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Article 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/1169/article/8)). AI can lay out a table you have already checked, and nothing more.
4. **AI never writes a review.** Since 6 April 2025 it has been unlawful to write, or to commission, a fake review: one that claims to be, but is not, based on a person's genuine experience. [The law](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20) says writing includes "creating by any means", so a review produced by AI is covered, as the [Competition and Markets Authority's guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews-cma208) sets out. Using an AI tool to help draft your own reply to a genuine review is a different thing. Check it and send it as yourself, and see [how to respond to bad reviews](/guides/how-to-respond-bad-pub-reviews).
5. **Pictures show what a guest will actually get.** The [advertising regulator says](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/disclosure-of-ai-in-advertising-striking-the-balance-between-creativity-and-responsibility.html) there is no general legal requirement in the UK to say that an advert used AI, and that its existing rules apply however an advert was made. A picture of food or of your pub must not mislead. Given the backlash the BBC reported, photographs of your own food and your own rooms avoid the question altogether.
6. **People decide about people.** Do not use an AI tool to choose who to hire, discipline or dismiss. For a question about a member of the team, BII members have an HR and Employment Law helpline; the number is on the [BII's helplines page](https://bii.org/resources-hub/helplines/).
7. **AI is a junior member of the team.** Treat it like one: it drafts, and you approve the final version.

Switch off training on whichever assistant you use:

| Assistant | Where the training switch is |
|---|---|
| ChatGPT | Settings, then Data controls: switch off "Improve the model for everyone" |
| Gemini | Activity: turn off "Keep activity" |
| Claude | Settings, then Privacy: switch off the option under "Help Improve our AI models" |
| Microsoft Copilot | No switch. Microsoft says prompts, responses and files in the Copilot app are not used to train its foundation models |

Claude also has incognito chats, on every plan, which are not kept in your history.

Write your rules on one page and go through them with the team. Here is a starting point to copy and adapt:

> **How we use AI at [PUB NAME]**
>
> 1. We use [NAME OF ASSISTANT] on the pub's account, not on personal accounts.
> 2. Nothing about a guest or a member of the team goes into it: no names, contact details, booking records, payroll or health information.
> 3. It never decides what is in a dish. Allergen information comes from the allergen chart and nowhere else.
> 4. It never writes a review, and nobody asks a friend or a member of the team to post one.
> 5. [NAMED PERSON] checks every date, price, opening time and claim before anything it wrote is used.
> 6. Pictures show what a guest will actually get: our food, our rooms, our people.
> 7. People decide about people. It is not used to choose who we hire, discipline or dismiss.
> 8. If something it produced goes wrong, tell [NAMED PERSON] the same day.

## Ask your suppliers what is already included

Before you pay for a new AI tool, talk to the suppliers you already pay: your till, your booking system, your rota app and your accounts package. Ask each of them three questions:

1. What AI features are included in what I already pay?
2. Is my data, or my guests' data, used to train your models?
3. Can I switch a feature off?

Ask for the answers in writing, and keep them with the contract. A feature you already pay for, switched on with care and covered by your house rules, may be all you need. If a supplier cannot say whether your guests' data trains its models, keep that data away from the feature until they can. The guide to [choosing a pub EPOS system](/guides/pub-epos-system-guide) covers what else to ask about a till.

## A first month

Four weeks, one step at a time:

1. **Week one: settings and house rules.** Switch off training, write your custom instructions and agree the one-page policy with the team.
2. **Week two: one job from the twelve.** Pick the one that takes most of your time now, use it every day, and check every answer before it goes anywhere.
3. **Week three: a weekly scheduled task.** Set up the Monday posts as a scheduled task, and edit what it drafts before anything is posted.
4. **Week four: see your pub the way AI does.** Ask the three questions from earlier in this guide, then fix what they throw up on your website and your Business Profile. Ask them again a month later and note what has changed.

Keep the weeks in that order. The settings and the rules come first because every step after them puts the pub's information into the assistant. When a prompt works, keep it in the project, so the whole team uses the same one rather than each person writing their own.

If you want a structured course alongside this, the BII's training pages list a free video course for members called [AI For Publicans](https://bii.org/training-course/ai-for-publicans/).

## Bring your own idea: how we use it

We have run tasting nights at The Anchor for years. The ideas are ours. AI does not come up with them, and it does not run the night by itself.

What it does now is the legwork. AI helps us turn each idea into a full plan, handle messages with suppliers, design the artwork and build an app to run the night, with guests rating each drink live on their own phones.

That is the lesson worth taking from it. Bring AI an idea you believe in and let it do the legwork. Asking it for the idea, and expecting it to run the night for you, gets the order the wrong way round.

More widely, at The Anchor, our own venue, we use AI for research, drafting, analysis and repetitive checking. We have no AI number: its contribution has not been measured separately from the wider work.

For an honest read on where AI would and would not pay in your business, try the [AI readiness assessment](/tools/ai-readiness), or read about [using AI intelligently](/growth-problems/using-ai-intelligently).
