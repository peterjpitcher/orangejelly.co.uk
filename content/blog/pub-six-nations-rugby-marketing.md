# Six Nations 2027 in Your Pub: Fixtures, Screening and Match-Day Ideas

The Six Nations is the biggest run of rugby in the first half of the year, and for a pub it is something rare: a reason for people to leave the sofa and watch together, on weekend after weekend, at times that suit a pub. In 2027 there are two championships to fill the pub with. The men's runs from Friday 5 February to Saturday 13 March, and the women's is played on five Saturdays from 10 April to 15 May.

What separates the pubs that do well from the pubs that just have the telly on is preparation: the fixtures on the wall early, a screen and sound that work, a match-day menu, bookings for the big games and a plan to bring the visitors back. Get those right and the Six Nations can introduce you to people who come back all year.

Rugby is not only a spring fixture. For the autumn internationals and the Nations Championship in November, see our [autumn rugby guide](/guides/autumn-rugby-nations-championship-pubs).

## Who shows it: every match is free to air

Every Six Nations match is on free-to-air television in the UK until 2029. ITV shows ten men's matches each year, including every England match. The BBC shows the other five, including every Scotland and Wales home match except when they host England, and the BBC shows every match of the women's Six Nations ([RFU, 14 March 2025](https://www.englandrugby.com/follow/news-media/six-nations-rugby-confirms-new-free-air-tv-deal-until-2029)).

That means you do not need a sports subscription to show the Six Nations. You do need a TV licence for the pub. Other rugby, such as club competitions, is on subscription channels, and showing a subscription channel needs a commercial subscription. Never show one in the pub on a domestic subscription: it breaks the subscription terms and can lead to legal action.

## Getting your screening right

You do not need a stadium-level AV system. You need reliability, a decent picture, and sound that works without deafening the people who did not come for the rugby.

### Screens or projectors

**Screens** are the safer choice for most pubs. A large commercial display is bright enough for a daylight room, does not need a dark space and needs little looking after. Mount it where the most seats can see it without neck strain. If your pub has more than one room, a second screen stops everybody crowding into one area while the rest sits empty.

**Projectors** give you a bigger picture but need a darker room and a proper screen or a flat white wall. They suit evening kick-offs and dedicated viewing areas, and three of the men's matches this year kick off at 8.10pm.

### Sound matters more than picture

One screen with clear sound beats three screens on mute. Use a decent soundbar or your existing speaker system, and test it before the first match. People will put up with an average picture, but not with commentary they cannot hear in a tight finish.

### Room layout for match days

Plan it before the first fixture, not during it.

- **Set a main viewing area** with the best screen angles, and arrange the seats so groups can see without standing.
- **Keep a room or a corner rugby-free** for people who came for a quiet drink. Pushing out your regulars to fit in match-day visitors is a poor trade.
- **Clear the tables before kick-off.** Match-day tables need room for drinks, food and elbows.
- **Leave standing room near the bar** for the people who come for the atmosphere.

## Match-day food and drink

Match-day offers should lift the spend per table, not cut your margin. The mistake is cheap pints: they fill the room and empty the till.

### Bundle, do not discount

Bundles feel generous to the guest and let you control the margin.

- **The match-day meal**: a burger, a pie or wings with a drink at a fixed price. Cost it against your target margin before you print it.
- **The sharing board**: loaded nachos, wings or chips for the table. Quick to make, a strong margin, and it keeps people in their seats at half-time.
- **A group deal for food**: a platter for four or six, booked with the table.

In Scotland, check any deal that includes alcohol with your licensing standards officer. Free or cheaper drinks when someone buys a drink, multi-buys such as a cheaper bucket of bottles, and alcohol as a prize are all banned, and a changed price must stay the same for at least 72 hours ([Licensing (Scotland) Act 2005, Schedule 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2005/16/schedule/3)). Never tie free or cheaper drinks to the score. In England and Wales, the [mandatory licence conditions](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2440/schedule/made) ban drinking games, and ban unlimited drinks for a fixed price and drinks as prizes where they risk undermining the licensing objectives.

### Drinks to push

Steer guests towards the serves with the best margin: premium lagers and good cask ale. Pitchers, where your licence and your local rules allow them, keep groups ordering from one server instead of queuing at the bar.

Do not forget the alcohol-free range. Groups bring drivers, and families come for the afternoon kick-offs, so a proper alcohol-free beer and real soft drinks show you have thought about everyone. Our guide to [low and no drinks](/guides/sober-october-low-no-alcohol-pubs) covers what to stock.

## Taking bookings for the big matches

For the biggest fixtures, you need bookings. In 2027 that means Ireland v England on the opening Friday, England v France on the Sunday of Valentine's Day, Wales v England on 6 March and the whole of Super Saturday on 13 March, with England v Scotland for the Calcutta Cup at 4.40pm. Walk-ins are fine for the quieter matches, but a packed pub with no reserved seats means complaints, wasted prep and regulars who cannot get a table.

### Keep it simple

You do not need new software. What matters is:

1. **Take a name and a phone number** for every booking.
2. **Confirm the match and the kick-off time**, so there is no confusion.
3. **Set expectations**: arrival time, how long the table is held, and any minimum spend.
4. **Send a reminder** the day before.

Confirmations and reminders were part of how we cut booking no-shows by 89% at The Anchor, our own venue. On a match day, an empty reserved table is a table you turned somebody else away from.

If you serve food, take food pre-orders with the booking. It speeds up the kitchen, cuts waste and means the table has committed to eating before it arrives.

### Know your capacity

Be honest about how many people your space holds comfortably for a match. Overselling makes a bad afternoon for everyone. If you sell out, you sell out: it is a good problem, and it is the reason to promote early booking for the next fixture.

## Promotion: a timeline that works

Start promoting about three weeks before the first match.

### The week of 11 January

Post the full fixture list with UK kick-off times. Use a clean graphic, not a wall of text, with your pub's name and location. This is the post local rugby fans share.

### The week of 18 January

Announce the match-day menu and the bundles. Show the food, the screen and the room on a busy day. If this is your first tournament, take photos of the screen during another match and use those.

### The week before each match

Open bookings, and be specific: "Tables for Ireland v England on Friday 5 February are going". Tell people exactly how to book.

### Match day

Post a short video of the atmosphere. It is the advert for the next fixture: people who see a packed, buzzing pub want to be part of it next time. Ask before you post anyone's face.

### The day after

Post the score, a crowd photo and the next fixture. Thank everyone who came.

For the women's championship, start again in mid-March: three matches every Saturday afternoon from 10 April makes a strong poster on its own. ## Turning match-day visitors into regulars

This is the part most pubs skip, and it is the most valuable part of the exercise. A busy Six Nations Saturday means little if those visitors never come back.

### Ask to stay in touch

Put a QR code on every table that asks one simple question: do you want to hear about the next match and our other events? Make it clear what they are signing up for, and only send marketing to the people who say yes. Offer them something that costs you nothing, such as first pick of the tables for the next big match.

### Follow up within two days

Send the people who signed up the next fixture and your other events: the quiz, the live music or the women's Six Nations. Speed matters. Wait a week and they have forgotten you.

### Give them a reason to return

Between match days you have midweek evenings to fill. Invite match-day visitors to your [quiz night](/guides/quiz-night-101) or a themed food evening, and mention your regular events at half-time or on the table cards. ## Planning beyond the Six Nations

The Six Nations is a template. What you learn on these match days applies to the autumn internationals, the men's Rugby World Cup in Australia in the autumn of 2027, and every other major sporting event you show.

Build a [seasonal events calendar](/guides/seasonal-pub-events-calendar) with every major fixture for the year, and plan your promotions, stock and staffing around those dates. If the Six Nations proves that live sport works for your pub, invest before the next tournament: a commercial-grade screen with an anti-glare coating if your current one struggles in daylight, and sound zones so the viewing area can be loud without overwhelming the rest of the pub.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Ignoring the people who are not watching.** Not everybody in on a Saturday afternoon wants rugby. Keep a space for them or lose them.

**Discounting instead of bundling.** Cheap pints destroy your margin. Bundle food and drink so the value is in the offer, not off the price.

**No bookings for the big matches.** Chaos at the door, overcrowding and guests who arrived early but cannot get a seat. Plan capacity and take bookings.

**Not asking to stay in touch.** Busy match days mean little if you have no way to invite those people back. The QR code is not optional.

**Poor sound.** A silent screen during a tense last five minutes is worse than no screen at all. Test the sound before the first match, and have a plan if it fails.

## The bottom line

The Six Nations 2027 is two championships of rugby that people want to watch together, with food and drink and atmosphere. Your job is to deliver that reliably, turn it into bookings and spend, and give the visitors a reason to come back long after the final whistle on Super Saturday.
