# 25 Low Budget Pub Marketing Ideas That Actually Work

Marketing does not need a big budget. It needs focus, local relevance and repetition. I run The Anchor in Stanwell Moor as a Greene King tenant, minutes from Heathrow and a short drive from a Wetherspoons, and nearly everything that has moved our numbers cost time rather than money.

Below are 25 ideas grouped into five pillars: visibility, social proof, partnerships, in-pub conversion and retention. A pub that is weak in one pillar leaks customers no matter how good the other four are.

Do not try all 25. Pick one from each pillar, run those five for six weeks, then add more. If you are starting from a standing start with nothing to spend, our [pub marketing](/pub-marketing) resource covers the free-first approach in detail.

## Pillar 1: Visibility in the community

**1. Post weekly in local Facebook groups, properly.** Read the pinned rules first. Most village and town groups ban straight adverts but allow event posts on a set day, or allow anything that is genuinely useful. Post from your personal profile rather than the page: people reply to a landlord, not a logo. A post that says "the road is shut at the bridge until Thursday, park round the back if you are coming for the quiz" reliably outperforms "quiz night 8pm", because the useful bit gets shared and your pub travels with it.

**2. Finish your Google Business Profile and then feed it.** Do this before anything else on the list, because it is what shows when someone searches "pub near me" or types your name, and it is free. Fill in every attribute: dog friendly, step-free access, beer garden, live sport, real ale, outside seating, car park. Add photos every week. Set holiday opening hours before every bank holiday. Answer the Q&A section yourself with the questions you get asked at the bar. Getting this right, alongside a proper website, is a large part of how we grew Google Search visibility by 828% at The Anchor. Our [Google Business Profile guide for pubs](/guides/google-business-profile-pub-guide) walks through each field.

**3. Make your A-board carry one message.** One offer, one date, one reason to come in. Three messages on a board is zero messages. Change it twice a week so regulars actually read it rather than walking past furniture.

**4. Get into the places locals already look.** Parish magazine, village noticeboard, community WhatsApp groups, the local CAMRA branch newsletter, school PTA newsletters, the sports club bar. These cost nothing but an email and a decent photo, and they reach the people most likely to walk to you.

**5. Keep your name, address and phone number identical everywhere.** Google, Facebook, TripAdvisor, your website, the brewery's pub finder. Mismatched details quietly damage how confidently search engines show you and make people think you have closed.

## Pillar 2: Social proof

**6. Share guest photos, with permission asked out loud.** "That looks great, mind if I put it on our Facebook?" takes three seconds and gets a yes almost every time. Tag the guest if they are happy with it, because their friends see it.

**7. Post a recap clip within 24 hours of every event.** Fifteen seconds of a full room and noise sells next month's event better than any poster. Post it while people still remember being there.

**8. Ask for reviews with a script, not a sign.** At the end of a good meal: "Really glad you enjoyed it. If you have a minute, a Google review makes a huge difference to us." Ask the tables who are clearly happy, not everyone.

**9. Reply to every review inside 48 hours.** Thank the good ones by name and reference what they had. Answer the bad ones once, calmly, with a fix. Future customers read your replies more carefully than the reviews.

**10. Print a small "what people say" card for tables.** Three real quotes and your Google rating. It works hardest on first-time visitors who are deciding whether to order food.

## Pillar 3: Partnerships

**11. Co-host with a local producer.** A nearby brewery, distillery, coffee roaster, baker or cheesemonger will often bring stock, staff and their own audience for a tasting night. You provide the room and the till.

**12. Give your quiet room away midweek.** Walking groups, running clubs, book clubs, knitting groups, model railway societies, parent-and-baby meet-ups. Free room, no minimum spend, one clear drinks and food offer. Twelve people on a dead Tuesday changes the feel of the whole pub.

**13. Run one charity event per quarter.** A Macmillan coffee morning, a village fund quiz, a sponsored anything. Local press and community groups will share a charity event when they will not share a promotion.

**14. Swap promotion with neighbouring businesses.** The barber, hairdresser, garage, garden centre and farm shop all have customers who live near you. Put their card on your noticeboard and ask for the same. No money changes hands.

**15. Ask your business development manager for support.** Point of sale, glassware, branded parasols, a brand activation, a raffle prize, staff training. Suppliers have budget for pubs that commit to a range and then actually sell it. You get nothing if you never ask.

## Pillar 4: In-pub conversion

**16. Put the weekly rhythm on every table.** A single card listing what happens on which night, all seven days. Most people in your pub tonight do not know you run a quiz.

**17. Build one hero bundle and name it.** Two courses plus a drink, or a sharing board plus a bottle. One bundle, priced clearly, easy for staff to say out loud.

**18. Make booking one tap.** A QR code on tables and the front door that goes straight to your booking page, not your homepage. Every extra tap loses people.

**19. Capture emails at the point of a sale, not with a fishbowl.** A booking form or a receipt with a clear marketing tick box is the cleanest route, because the UK soft opt-in only reaches addresses you collected during a sale or negotiations for one. A fishbowl of business cards gives you a pile of addresses you cannot legally mail.

**20. Train one sentence at the till.** "Are you coming Wednesday for the quiz?" asked by every member of staff, every shift. It is the cheapest promotion in the building and it needs no printing.

## Pillar 5: Retention

**21. Run a loyalty card that rewards frequency, not spend.** Five visits, sixth drink on the house. Rewarding frequency brings people back on quiet nights, which is where you need them.

**22. Send one short email a week.** What is on, one photo, one link to book. Short beats clever. Our guide to [email marketing for pub retention](/guides/email-marketing-pub-retention) covers structure and timing.

**23. Use a WhatsApp broadcast list, never a group.** A broadcast sends one message to many people privately. A group turns into forty notifications and everyone leaves.

**24. Personally invite your top thirty regulars to anything new.** A text from you beats a poster. These are the people who bring three others.

**25. Note birthdays and anniversaries.** A card behind the bar and a free round on the night creates loyalty that discounting never buys.

## What £50 a month actually buys

If you do have a small budget, spend it in this order. First, better photos: an hour with a local photographer or a phone tripod and a window seat at golden hour. Second, printing: table cards and a decent A-board pen set. Third, and only once your organic posts are consistent, £10 to £20 boosting the one post each month that is already performing. Never boost a post that flopped organically.

Marketing spend is the last thing to cut and the last thing to add. Get the free work running first, then amplify what is already working.

## Common mistakes

**Trying all 25 at once.** Five ideas run for six weeks beat twenty-five run for four days. Pick one per pillar and hold your nerve.

**Posting without a call to action.** A lovely photo of a roast with no time, no price and no booking link is a postcard, not marketing. Every post needs one clear next step.

**No follow-up after a good night.** Sixty people in on Saturday and nothing said to them on Sunday is the most expensive mistake on this list. Post the recap, thank the room, name the next date.

## How to run the six weeks

Write the five ideas you picked on the office wall with the date you started. Review them against one number and pick that number now: covers, wet sales or bookings. Keep what moved it, drop what did not, add the next five.

Change one thing at a time inside those six weeks. If you swap the posting rhythm, the quiz night and the table bundle in the same fortnight, the number will move and you will have no idea which of the three did it, which means you cannot repeat it.

Tell the team what you are testing and why. Ideas 6, 8 and 20 on this list all depend on somebody saying a sentence out loud to a stranger, and that only happens if they know it is the plan rather than your latest enthusiasm.

## Mini FAQ

**Should I be on every platform?**
No. Two channels done every week beat five done occasionally, and for most pubs that is Facebook plus your Google Business Profile. Our [social media strategy for pubs](/guides/social-media-strategy-for-pubs) covers how to pick your two and how to keep them going through a busy month.
