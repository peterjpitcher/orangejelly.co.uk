# Monday Night Pub Promotions That Actually Work (Not Another Quiz Night)

Monday is hard because people are tired, cautious with money, and not looking for a loud night. The instinct is to copy what works on a Friday, only cheaper. That instinct is what kills most Monday promotions.

The pubs that crack Monday do something different. They give a specific group of people one easy reason to leave the house, at a time that suits them, at a price that still makes money. No spectacle, no big spend, no discounting your way to a busy room full of people spending nothing.

I run The Anchor in Stanwell Moor as a Greene King tenant, and here is how I would build a Monday from scratch: the arithmetic, the legal checks, and the mistakes that cost me time before I worked them out.

## The Monday rule: keep it simple

Before you pick a format, work out what Monday actually has to clear. Most licensees never do this, which is why they cannot tell a good Monday from a bad one.

Take a worked example. Say your Monday session costs £190 in wages once you add employer National Insurance and holiday accrual, plus roughly £40 in heat, light and cellar cooling that you would not burn if the doors stayed shut. That is £230 of gross profit you need before you have made a penny.

At a blended gross profit of 65%, which is where most mixed wet and dry trade sits, £230 of GP needs about £354 in net sales. If your average spend per head is £14 net of VAT, which is around £16.80 over the till, that is roughly 26 people through the door. Not 100. Twenty-six.

That number changes everything. You do not need a spectacular event. You need one reliable reason for 26 to 40 people to turn up, week in, week out. Run your own figures before you plan anything, because the gap between "Monday feels dead" and "Monday loses money" is often smaller than you think.

## Four formats that actually work

**1) Comfort menu night**

One hero dish at a fixed price, promoted by name. Pie night, curry night, burger and chips. The kitchen runs a short prep list and the guest does not have to think.

Watch what a bundle does to your margin. Say you sell a pie and a pint at £13.50 including VAT. That is £11.25 net. If the pie costs you £3.10 in ingredients and the pint costs £1.90, your gross profit is £6.25, which is 55.6% of net sales. That is roughly nine points below a normal trading night.

The bundle only wins on volume, and the volume needed is bigger than it looks. Twenty covers at £14 net spend on 65% GP gives you £182 of gross profit. On the bundle you make £6.25 a head, so you need thirty covers just to match that, and thirty-four to be genuinely ahead at £212.50. So the question is not "does the offer feel generous", it is "will it bring me half as many people again". Cost it out before you print it, and pick a hero dish with a genuinely low food cost so the compression is bearable. Our guide to [menu engineering that lifts average spend](/guides/menu-engineering-lift-average-spend) covers how to choose that dish properly.

**2) Community club night**

You provide the space, they bring the audience. Walking groups, book clubs, knitting and craft groups, model railway societies, running clubs looking for a post-run pint, choirs, D&D and wargaming groups, local history societies. Every town has a dozen of these hunting for a warm room.

Approach them with a proper offer: a fixed slot every Monday, a named room, a contact who is always on shift, and no hire fee. In return ask for a realistic minimum spend or simply agree that everyone buys a drink. Put their meeting in your diary as a recurring booking so nobody double-books the space.

The mistake is handing over your best snug to twelve people drinking tea. Give them the space you were not going to fill, tell them what time you need the room back, and get their organiser to add your pub name to their WhatsApp group and Facebook page.

**3) Low-key entertainment**

Board games, cards, an acoustic session in the corner. Nothing that needs a stage or a PA. A [board game night runs on almost nothing](/guides/boardgame-night-101) once you have twenty decent games in a cupboard, and it holds people for three hours rather than one.

**4) Early week treat**

A small indulgence rather than a meal: dessert and a coffee bundle, a three-glass wine flight, a cheeseboard for two. This suits pubs where Monday food is not viable but the bar is open anyway. Margins on wine and desserts are usually kinder than on a main course, so a treat night can out-earn a food night on lower covers.

| Format | Setup cost | Extra staff | Weeks to build | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comfort menu night | Low, menu print only | Kitchen cover | 6 to 8 | Margin compression from bundling |
| Community club night | None | None | 2 to 4 | Low spend per head |
| Low-key entertainment | £100 to £250 of games | None | 6 to 10 | Long dwell, small round sizes |
| Early week treat | Low | None | 4 to 6 | Too narrow an audience |

## Price it so it still makes money

Discounting is the reflex and it is usually the wrong one. A 20% cut on drinks hands margin to the people who were coming anyway. A bundle at a fixed price gives you a headline number to shout about while keeping control of what goes in it.

Three rules I would hold to. Fix the price rather than the percentage, so guests remember it. Include one item you have real margin on, usually a soft drink, a coffee or a house wine. And never let the offer run all week, because the whole point is that Monday is the only night it exists.

## The legal bits to check before you print the poster

Four things worth checking once, then forgetting about.

**Permitted price.** You cannot sell alcohol below the duty on that product plus the VAT charged on that duty. It is a mandatory condition on every premises licence in England and Wales. Deep drinks discounting on cheap products is where pubs trip over this.

**Irresponsible promotions.** The mandatory conditions also ban promotions that encourage speed or volume drinking: drinking games, unlimited-drink offers for a fixed price, and anything that rewards drinking against the clock. A quiz with a bar tab prize is fine. "Beat the barrel" is not.

**Music.** If customers can hear music, whether that is a playlist, a TV, a jukebox or a picture round with song clips, you need TheMusicLicence from PPL PRS. Cost varies with your size and hours.

**Live music and gaming.** The Live Music Act 2012 removes most licence conditions on live music in licensed premises between 8am and 11pm for audiences up to 500, which covers almost any Monday acoustic set. Gaming is fussier, and the two formats pubs reach for sit under different rules. Card games such as poker fall under exempt gaming in alcohol-licensed premises: you cannot charge a participation fee, and stake and prize limits apply. Bingo has its own regime, and you have to tell the Gambling Commission if stakes or prizes exceed £2,000 in any seven-day period. Both sets of limits are published in the Gambling Commission's guidance, so read it there rather than copying what the pub down the road does.

## Marketing rhythm

Promotion is not one poster. It is a repeatable weekly loop that takes about fifteen minutes.

- **Sunday, late afternoon.** One post while people are planning their week. Show the food, name the price, say the time.
- **Monday, lunchtime.** A second post with availability. "Four tables left for pie night" beats "don't forget pie night".
- **Monday, all shift.** A bar script. Every member of staff mentions next Monday to anyone who is in tonight.
- **All week.** The A-board and a table talker carry the same wording. Consistency is what turns an event into a habit.
- **Every month.** A Google Business Profile post and one message to your opted-in list.

Take bookings, even when you do not need to. A named night people can reserve a table for behaves completely differently from a general invitation to pop in. We grew table bookings at The Anchor by 403%, and a large part of that was simply giving people specific occasions worth booking rather than a vague welcome.

## How to tell if it is working

Judge Monday on four numbers, reviewed at week six.

1. **Covers or head count**, against the break-even figure you calculated at the start.
2. **Net sales and blended GP**, so you can see whether the bundle is doing damage.
3. **Spend per head**, which tells you if you have attracted drinkers or lingerers.
4. **Repeat names**, counted from the booking sheet. This is the real signal. A format with 30 people and no repeats is a stunt; 22 people with 14 repeats is a business.

Also check Tuesday and Wednesday have not dropped. If Monday grew by 25 covers and Tuesday lost 20, you have moved trade, not created it.

## Common mistakes

- **Building a complex event on a low-energy night.** If it needs a compere, a PA and three staff, it is a Friday format.
- **Discounting instead of bundling.** A price cut trains regulars to wait for the offer. A bundle keeps a floor under your margin.
- **Promoting once, then going quiet.** The same wording, in the same places, in the same order every week is what turns a night into something people remember without checking.
- **Cancelling a quiet week.** One cancellation tells the eight people who did turn up that you were not serious. Run it with four people if you have to.
- **Changing the format every month.** Give it a fair run, then change one element, not all of them.
- **No booking route.** If the only way in is to turn up and hope, you have no data and no forecast.

## Quick checklist

- Break-even covers calculated for a Monday session.
- One format chosen, and only one.
- Offer costed to a net GP you can live with.
- Licence, music and gaming checks done.
- Team briefed with a one-line bar script.
- Sunday and Monday posts scheduled for the next six weeks.
- Booking route live, with a way to record repeat names.

## Mini FAQ

**Should I run a quiz on Monday?**
Only if your audience already wants one and nobody nearby has claimed the night. A quiz needs a good host and a decent crowd to feel alive, and a thin quiz is worse than no quiz.

**What if Monday still loses money after a fair run at it?**
Then close on Mondays and put the hours into a night that works. That is a legitimate answer, and it is the one I would rather you reached deliberately than by drifting.
