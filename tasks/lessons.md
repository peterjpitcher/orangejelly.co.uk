# Lessons

Patterns from Peter's corrections. Read at the start of a session.

## 17 October 2026: never pick a brand asset by its file name

**What happened:** for the Greene King pack I supplied `public/logo.png` because of its name.
It was the 2025 "OJ" logo; the current orange-slice logo lives in `public/brand/`.

**Pattern:** a file called `logo.png` at the root looks canonical and was not. Old assets stay
in a repo long after a rebrand.

**How to apply:** take brand assets from what the live site renders (the header uses
`public/brand/logo-horizontal.png`), and look at the image before supplying it. The old logo
files were deleted on 17 October 2026 so this cannot recur by accident.

## 17 October 2026: write as Peter

**What happened:** a note to Charlotte that Peter sends under his name said "a photo of Peter
for the byline".

**Pattern:** anything that goes out from Peter (cover notes, notes to a client or designer,
emails, an invitation in his article) is written in his first person: "a photo of me",
"call me".

**How to apply:** before handing over any document Peter will send, read it as if he wrote it.
Repository documentation and handover notes for agents stay in the third person.

## 16 October 2026: write for eager readers

**What happened:** for the Greene King spring toolkit piece I suggested inviting businesses
"open to change". Peter changed it to "ready for change": "I want people who are eager".

**Pattern:** an Orange Jelly invitation should pick out owners who are keen to act, not
reassure ones who are hesitant.

**How to apply:** write "ready for change" in new promotional copy, in print and in guides.
The live site still says "if you are open to change"; leave it unless Peter asks.

## 22 September 2026: do not cap what respondents can say

**What happened:** the first survey's two app screens were built as "pick up to three". Peter
asked for "as many as they'd like".

**Pattern:** when the job is research, a limit on answers is a product decision that throws
away data, and it was not asked for. Default to letting people pick everything that applies,
and get the forced choice from a separate "if you could only have one" question instead.

**How to apply:** before adding a cap, minimum, required field or cut-off to anything a
customer answers, check it was asked for. If it seems useful, propose it rather than build it.

## 5 September 2026: make the work explicit

Peter clarified that Orange Jelly specialises in websites, bespoke applications and connected systems, using AI to drive business growth. Do not let broad consultancy keyword volume redefine that offer. Lead with concrete delivery and use strategy as the method. Keep hospitality as strong evidence rather than the entire company identity.
