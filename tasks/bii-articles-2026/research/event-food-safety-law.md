## 0. How to read this dossier

- **Type** is one of LAW (statute or assimilated regulation), GUIDANCE (regulator or public body advice) or GOOD PRACTICE (trade body or researcher's reading, not a regulator's requirement).
- **Status** is VERIFIED (read at the primary source on 13 October 2026), SECONDARY (trade press or newspaper only) or UNVERIFIED (not found, not accessible, or researcher's inference).
- Quotes are exact and under 40 words. Where the source says "should", this dossier says "should".
- No em dashes are used. Some quotes are cut short to avoid dashes in the original.

## 1. Registration and telling the council about changes

**1.1 [LAW, all nations] Every food business must register each establishment it controls with the local authority.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: legislation.gov.uk, Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 Article 6(2), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/852/article/6
Quote: "every food business operator shall notify the appropriate competent authority, in the manner that the latter requires, of each establishment under its control that carries out any of the stages of production, processing and distribution of food"

**1.2 [LAW, all nations] The business must keep the authority up to date, including any significant change in activities.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: as 1.1, Article 6(2) second subparagraph.
Quote: "Food business operators shall also ensure that the competent authority always has up-to-date information on establishments, including by notifying any significant change in activities and any closure of an existing establishment."

**1.3 [LAW, England] Failing to comply is a criminal offence.**
Status: VERIFIED. Sources: legislation.gov.uk, Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013 regulation 19, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/2996/regulation/19 ; FSA, Food Law Practice Guidance (England), issued October 2025, section A2.2.8, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a3d498d4c7605ab56723b4b/Food_Law_Practice_Guidance__England__-_25-6-26.pdf
Quotes: regulation 19(1): "any person who contravenes or fails to comply with any of the specified EU provisions commits an offence." Practice Guidance: "Not complying with this requirement is an offence under regulation 19 of The Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013."
Note: the Practice Guidance sentence is written about notifying a change of ownership under Article 6(2). Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have equivalent offence provisions in their own regulations (not quoted here).

**1.4 [GUIDANCE, England; equivalent documents exist for Wales and NI] "Significant change" is not defined in Article 6. The FSA tells councils that it includes changes in food activities.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: FSA, Food Law Practice Guidance (England), issued October 2025, section A2.2.7 (URL as 1.3).
Quote: "Significant changes are considered to include changes in, or ceasing of:" followed by three bullets: "food activities", "ownership", "changes to the details previously supplied".

**1.5 [GUIDANCE, England, Wales, NI] The FSA tells businesses they should inform the council of changes to registerable food activities and of changes that add risk.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: FSA, Starting a food business (section "Changing your business operating model"), GOV.UK, published 10 January 2023, last updated 19 August 2026, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/starting-a-food-business
Quotes: "You should inform your local authority of any changes to your registerable food activities. This includes the introduction of a new delivery or takeaway service." Also: "should be informed of any changes that may introduce additional risks into your food production processes."
The same page: "You must make sure your local authority has up to date information about your food business."

**1.6 [GUIDANCE, England and Wales] The SFBB pack states it as a "must".**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: FSA, Safer food, better business: Introduction (PDF, undated), https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69b1a53ccdd628b29e349707/sfbb-introduction_2.pdf (parent page published 5 June 2025, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/safer-food-better-business-sfbb)
Quote: "You must also tell your local council about any significant changes to your business on an ongoing basis."

**1.7 [GUIDANCE, England, Wales, NI] The one activity the FSA names in terms as a significant change is serving burgers less than thoroughly cooked.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: FSA, Less than thoroughly cooked beef burgers: guidance for food businesses, section 2.2, GOV.UK, published 22 May 2023, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/less-than-thoroughly-cooked-beef-burgers-guidance-for-food-businesses-and-local-authorities
Quote: "they are legally required to inform their LA because it constitutes a significant change to their business operation."

**1.8 Whether buffets, barbecues, hog roasts, outside catering or off-site events count as a significant change.**
Status: UNVERIFIED as a stated rule. No FSA, FSS or legislative text was found that names these activities. What is verified: the law requires notice of "any significant change in activities" (1.2); the FSA treats changes in "food activities" as significant (1.4); and the FSA says councils "should be informed of any changes that may introduce additional risks" (1.5). Researcher's reading (GOOD PRACTICE): a pub registered for a standard menu that starts outside catering, off-site events or hog roasts is changing its food activities and adding risk, so it should tell the council in writing. A buffet in the pub's own function room is a weaker case. The council's environmental health team decides. Local authority wording on this point could not be searched (see section 16).

**1.9 [GUIDANCE with one legal element] The 28-day period.**
Status: VERIFIED that it is in guidance. Article 6(2) itself contains no time period.
- FSA, Food business registration, GOV.UK, published 31 August 2023, last updated 25 June 2026, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/food-business-registration : "You are required to register at least 28 days before trading."
- FSA, Starting a food business (URL as 1.5): "You should do this at least 28 days before you start trading." and "Registration of your food business is free and can't be refused."
- FSA, Food Law Practice Guidance (England), A2.1.2: "FBOs are advised to submit a registration of a food establishment at least 28 days before the business starts trading or the food operations commence."
- Scotland: FSS, Registering a food business (no date shown), https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/business-guidance/starting-a-new-business/registering-a-food-business : "All food businesses must register with the environmental health service at their local authority at least 28 days before opening."
Note for print: the sources use "required", "should" and "advised" for the same 28 days. Quote the one you rely on.

**1.10 [GUIDANCE, England, Wales, NI] Each location is registered with its own council; mobile units register where they are kept.**
Status: VERIFIED. Sources: FSA, Food business registration (URL as 1.9); FSA Practice Guidance (England) A2.5.1 and A2.6.6.
Quotes: "If you operate in more than one location, you need to register each site with the local authority in which they are located." Practice Guidance A2.5.1: mobile operators register with the authority "within which the establishment is ordinarily kept or returns to between trading." A2.6.6 on pop-ups: "These businesses are treated in the same way as mobile food establishments for the purposes of registration."

**1.11 [GUIDANCE, England] A one-off event is not automatically exempt.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: FSA Practice Guidance (England) A2.2.1.
Quote: "if an activity occurs only once or only low risk food is handled, it does not necessarily mean it is exempt from registration."

## 2. Food safety management based on HACCP

**2.1 [LAW, all nations] Permanent HACCP-based procedures are required.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: legislation.gov.uk, Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 Article 5(1), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/852/article/5
Quote: "Food business operators shall put in place, implement and maintain a permanent procedure or procedures based on the HACCP principles."

**2.2 [LAW, all nations] The procedures must be reviewed whenever the product, process or any step changes.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: as 2.1, Article 5(2) final sentence.
Quote: "When any modification is made in the product, process, or any step, food business operators shall review the procedure and make the necessary changes to it."

**2.3 [LAW, all nations] Documents and records are required, in proportion to the business, and must be kept up to date.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: as 2.1, Article 5(2)(g) and 5(4).
Quotes: Article 5(2)(g): "establishing documents and records commensurate with the nature and size of the food business". Article 5(4)(b): "ensure that any documents describing the procedures developed in accordance with this Article are up-to-date at all times". Article 5(4)(c): "retain any other documents and records for an appropriate period."

- SFBB Introduction (URL as 1.6): "This pack is for small catering businesses such as restaurants, cafés and takeaways." and "Other options as well as designing your own are the FSA's Safe Catering Pack or the MyHACCP tool which is better suited to more complex food businesses."
- Northern Ireland: Safe Catering. ## 3. Temperature control: law and guidance by nation

### 3A. England, Wales and Northern Ireland: the law

The three nations have near-identical wording. England: Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013, Schedule 4, given effect by regulation 32 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/2996/schedule/4). Wales: Food Hygiene (Wales) Regulations 2006, Schedule 4, given effect by regulation 30 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2006/31/schedule/4). Northern Ireland: Food Hygiene Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2006, Schedule 4, given effect by regulation 27 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisr/2006/3/schedule/4). All VERIFIED. Paragraph numbers are the same in all three.

**3.1 [LAW] Cold holding limit: 8°C.** Schedule 4 paragraph 2(1). It applies to food "likely to support the growth of pathogenic micro-organisms or the formation of toxins". England quote: "at or in food premises at a temperature above 8 °C commits an offence." (Wales: "will be guilty of an offence"; NI: "shall be guilty of an offence".)

**3.2 [LAW] The single period out of chill control: less than four hours, once.** Schedule 4 paragraph 5(1). It is a defence to prove the food "(a) was for service or on display for sale", "(b) had not previously been kept for service or on display for sale at a temperature above 8 °C", and "(c) had been kept for service or on display for sale for a period of less than four hours."
Note: the statute says "less than four hours". FSA guidance says "up to four hours".

**3.3 [LAW] Other limited periods above 8°C.** Schedule 4 paragraph 5(2): a defence where food was above 8°C "for an unavoidable reason", such as "to accommodate the practicalities of handling during and after processing or preparation", "the defrosting of equipment" or "temporary breakdown of equipment", and only "for a limited period only and that period was consistent with food safety."

**3.4 [LAW] Hot holding minimum: 63°C.** Schedule 4 paragraph 6: an offence to keep "at a temperature below 63 °C any food which (a) has been cooked or reheated; (b) is for service or on display for sale; and (c) needs to be kept at or above 63 °C". (Quote joins the lettered sub-paragraphs, which appear on separate lines in the original.)

**3.5 [LAW] The single period below 63°C: less than two hours, once.** Schedule 4 paragraph 7(2): a defence to prove the food "(a) had been kept for service or on display for sale for a period of less than two hours; and (b) had not previously been kept for service or on display for sale by that person."

**3.6 [LAW] The exemptions are limited.** Schedule 4 paragraph 3 exempts, among others, "food which, for the duration of its shelf life may be kept at ambient temperatures with no risk to health". Paragraph 7(1) allows a lower hot holding temperature only where "a well-founded scientific assessment" supports it.

### 3B. Scotland: the law is different

Food Hygiene (Scotland) Regulations 2006, Schedule 4, given effect by regulation 30, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2006/3/schedule/4 . VERIFIED.

**3.7 [LAW] No temperature figure for cold holding.** Schedule 4 paragraph 2(1): food must not be kept otherwise than "(a) in a refrigerator or refrigerating chamber or in a cool ventilated place; or (b) at a temperature above 63°C".

**3.8 [LAW] Hot holding is "above 63°C".** Same paragraph. England, Wales and NI say "at or above 63°C".

**3.9 [LAW] No four-hour or two-hour tolerance periods in the Scottish Schedule. Instead there are exemptions.** Schedule 4 paragraph 2(2) disapplies paragraph 2(1) for food "(a) which is undergoing preparation for sale", "(b) which is exposed for sale or has been sold to a consumer whether for immediate consumption or otherwise", and (c) food "being cooled under hygienic conditions as quickly as possible to a temperature which would not result in a risk to health".
Consequence: a Scottish buffet on display is not caught by paragraph 2(1), but the general rule in 3.11 still applies.

**3.10 [LAW] Reheating: not less than 82°C, with a quality defence.** Schedule 4 paragraph 3(1): reheated food "shall, on being reheated, be raised to a temperature of not less than 82°C." Paragraph 3(3): "it shall be a defence for the accused to prove that the food could not have been raised to a temperature of not less than 82°C without a deterioration of its qualities."

### 3C. All four nations: the general rules

Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 Annex II Chapter IX, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/852/annex/II . VERIFIED.

**3.11 [LAW] No temperatures that risk health; limited periods out of control allowed.** Paragraph 5: "The cold chain is not to be interrupted." It continues: "limited periods outside temperature control are permitted, to accommodate the practicalities of handling during preparation, transport, storage, display and service of food, provided that it does not result in a risk to health."

**3.12 [LAW] Cooling: as quickly as possible. No time limit in law.** Paragraph 6: "they are to be cooled as quickly as possible following the heat-processing stage, or final preparation stage if no heat process is applied, to a temperature which does not result in a risk to health."

**3.13 [LAW] Thawing. No figures in law.** Paragraph 7: "The thawing of foodstuffs is to be undertaken in such a way as to minimise the risk of growth of pathogenic microorganisms or the formation of toxins in the foods."

**3.14 There is no statutory cooking temperature in any UK nation.** Status: VERIFIED by reading the four Schedules and Annex II; the only statutory heating figure found is Scotland's 82°C for reheating. All cooking figures below are GUIDANCE.

### 3D. The official explanation of the difference

### 3E. Guidance figures

**7.10 Legal responsibility where a venue brings in or allows an outside caterer.**
Status: the legal texts are VERIFIED (7.1, 7.2); the application below is the researcher's reading, UNVERIFIED by any regulator statement.
- Where the venue buys the food and sells it to guests as part of its package, the venue is placing that food on the market. It carries Article 14 and Article 17 duties for it and relies on due diligence (7.2).
- Where the caterer contracts with the customer and merely uses the venue's space, the caterer is the food business operator for its own food. The venue's duties continue for its own premises, its own food and anything it does to the caterer's food (storing, reheating, serving).
- In the one event-catering prosecution found (4.4), the caterer was prosecuted, not the venue.
- No FSA or FSS page was found that states how responsibility divides between venue and outside caterer. Print this as "ask your environmental health officer", not as settled.

Researcher's reading (GOOD PRACTICE): a family that brings its own food is not a food business. The venue is not the operator for food it does not supply, but it becomes responsible for what its staff do with that food, and its own kitchen controls still apply. A written house policy and a word with the insurer are sensible. This is not a regulator's statement.

## 8. High-risk foods and vulnerable guests

**8.1 [GUIDANCE, England and Wales] Foods the FSA says need extra care.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: SFBB Foods that need extra care, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69c53961471d520038d0f6da/sfbb-caterer-cooking-02-foods-that-need-extra-care_0.pdf

## 9. Staff: fitness to work, supervision and training

**9.1 [LAW, all nations] Exclusion of ill food handlers.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 Annex II Chapter VIII paragraph 2, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/852/annex/II
The paragraph covers anyone "suffering from, or being a carrier of a disease likely to be transmitted through food" or with "infected wounds, skin infections, sores or diarrhoea". Such a person is not "to be permitted to handle food or enter any food-handling area in any capacity if there is any likelihood of direct or indirect contamination."

**9.2 [LAW, all nations] Duty to report illness.** Same paragraph: "Any person so affected and employed in a food business and who is likely to come into contact with food is to report immediately the illness or symptoms, and if possible their causes, to the food business operator." VERIFIED.

**9.3 [LAW, all nations] Cleanliness and clothing apply to everyone working in a food-handling area.** Chapter VIII paragraph 1: "Every person working in a food-handling area is to maintain a high degree of personal cleanliness and is to wear suitable, clean and, where necessary, protective clothing." VERIFIED. The wording is "every person working", not "every employee".

**9.4 [GUIDANCE] The 48-hour rule is guidance, not statute.**
Status: VERIFIED.
- FSA, Fitness to work, GOV.UK, published 29 April 2025, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fitness-to-work : "Managers must exclude staff with these symptoms from working with or around open food, normally for 48 hours from when symptoms stop naturally."
- GOV.UK (FSA), Running a food business, part "Staff training, illness and hygiene", last updated 30 January 2025, https://www.gov.uk/running-food-business : "Staff with diarrhoea or vomiting must not return to work until they have had no symptoms for 48 hours."
- SFBB Personal hygiene and fitness to work (in the caterers' pack): "Even if the diarrhoea and vomiting has stopped, someone can still carry harmful bacteria for 48 hours afterwards."
- Scotland: FSS, Food Handlers: Fitness to work, December 2019, https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/business-guidance/running-a-food-business/publications/food-handlers-fitness-to-work-best-practice-advice-for-food-businesses : exclude until "they are no longer infectious (48 hours from when symptoms stopped naturally)."
The Annex II text (9.1) contains no number of hours.

**9.5 [GUIDANCE] Household contacts and sick pay.** GOV.UK Running a food business: "Staff must also tell their manager if anyone they live with has diarrhoea or vomiting." FSA Fitness to work: "Penalising staff for being ill, for example by not paying them when they are excluded from work, could lead to them working whilst ill and may lead to food safety problems." VERIFIED.

**9.6 [LAW, all nations] Supervision, instruction and training.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: Annex II Chapter XII.
Quotes: operators are to ensure "that food handlers are supervised and instructed and/or trained in food hygiene matters commensurate with their work activity". And that those responsible for the HACCP procedure "have received adequate training in the application of the HACCP principles".

**9.7 [GUIDANCE] How the FSA words it (stronger than the statute's text).**
Status: VERIFIED. Sources: FSA, Managing food safety (URL at 2.10); GOV.UK Running a food business.
Quotes: "You must make sure that any member of staff who handles food is trained in food hygiene and safety, including allergens, before they start work." And: "You must be able to show that your staff have the right skills and up-to-date training for their role." And: "Food handlers do not have to hold a food hygiene certificate."
Print carefully: the statute says "supervised and instructed and/or trained ... commensurate with their work activity". "Before they start work" is the FSA's guidance wording.

**9.8 [GUIDANCE] Temporary, agency and casual staff.**
Status: VERIFIED where quoted; otherwise the researcher's reading.
- The legal duties attach to "food handlers" and "every person working in a food-handling area" (9.3, 9.6). No exemption for agency, casual or volunteer staff appears in the text.
- Scotland: FSS Fitness to work: "These individuals may be employed directly by the business or through an agency."
- England and Wales: SFBB Introduction: "Use the 'Working with food?' factsheet to train your staff on good personal hygiene on their first day at work." SFBB Training and supervision: "You should train your staff in all the safe methods contained within this toolkit that are relevant to the job they do."
- Scotland: CookSafe Training house rules (page dated 1 May 2016): "Before starting work for the first time, it is good practice that all food handling staff should have received either written or verbal instruction on 'The Essentials of Food Hygiene'".
- The FSA's fitness to work page offers a "Fitness to work questionnaire: Pre-employment, visitor, returning from abroad".
- No FSA text specific to volunteers working for a commercial venue was found. For community groups the FSA says: "You do not need a food hygiene certificate to make and sell food for charity events."

**9.9 [GUIDANCE, Northern Ireland] Food safety culture, including temporary staff.**
Status: VERIFIED as an FSA statement. Source: FSA in Northern Ireland, Safe Catering pack supplement: Food safety culture (undated; listed at https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/safe-catering on 13 October 2026), https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a992e049a177a1decf97d25/Safe_Catering_NI_-_Food_Safety_Culture.pdf
Quotes: culture covers "owners and managers to food handlers, kitchen staff, servers, cleaners and temporary/agency staff". The supplement says food safety culture "is required by food law."
Nation difference: the Great Britain text of Annex II on legislation.gov.uk contains no food safety culture chapter (checked). The researcher understands the Northern Ireland requirement to come from Commission Regulation (EU) 2021/382; that instrument was not opened in this session.

## 10. Food hygiene ratings

**10.1 [GUIDANCE, England, Wales, NI] What is assessed.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: FSA, Food Hygiene Rating Scheme (FHRS): guidance for businesses, GOV.UK, published 14 February 2018, last updated 30 June 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/food-hygiene-rating-scheme-fhrs-guidance-for-businesses
Three elements. The third: "How you manage ways of keeping food safe, looking at processes, training, and systems to ensure good hygiene is maintained. The officer can then assess the level of confidence in standards being maintained in the future."
And: "To get the top rating, you must do well in all three elements".

**10.2 [GUIDANCE, England; Wales and NI use statutory equivalents] How documentation feeds the score.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: FSA, FHRS Brand Standard, Revision 8, June 2023 (URL at 7.4).
- Paragraph 3.8: "One of the factors to be taken into account includes satisfactory HACCP-based procedures and they cannot be termed 'satisfactory' (in terms of maintaining compliance) if they are not being properly implemented."
- Table 3 mapping (lower scores are better): total 0 to 15 with no single score above 5 gives a rating of 5; total 20, none above 10, gives 4; total 25 to 30, none above 10, gives 3; total 35 to 40, none above 15, gives 2; total 45 to 50, none above 20, gives 1; over 50 gives 0.
- Table 4 example: scores of "5, 5, 20" give a rating of 1. One bad "confidence in management" score pulls the whole rating down.
- Documentation is proportionate: "The level of documentation will vary between businesses depending on the types and complexity of operations being undertaken".
- FSA Food Law Code of Practice (England), issued 23 October 2025, Annex 1 Part 3 (food hygiene): "The confidence in management score should assess whether a business' food safety management/control procedures are appropriate, with the identification of the correct hazards and controls." And: "It is to elicit a judgement on the likelihood of satisfactory compliance being maintained in the future." (https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a3d4a1230b491f55b3c4a81/Food_Law_Code_of_Practice__England_.pdf)
- The same Code, in its separate food standards compliance assessment (Table 2, "Management" factor, which does not feed the hygiene rating), lists: "any changes to activities since last visit have been reflected in the management system".
Reading (GOOD PRACTICE): a venue that adds events but leaves its pack unchanged has procedures that no longer identify "the correct hazards and controls", which is what the hygiene score assesses.

**10.3 Display: nation by nation.**
- England [GUIDANCE]: voluntary. FSA guidance (10.1): "You can display your rating in your premises' window or door so that customers can see it easily." VERIFIED.
- Wales [LAW]: Food Hygiene Rating (Wales) Act 2013 section 7(1), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2013/2/section/7 : "the operator must display the food hygiene rating sticker provided." VERIFIED.
- Northern Ireland [LAW]: Food Hygiene Rating Act (Northern Ireland) 2016 section 7(1), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nia/2016/3/section/7 : the operator "must ensure that a valid sticker showing the establishment's food hygiene rating is displayed in the location and manner specified". VERIFIED.
- Summary line: GOV.UK (FSA), Food hygiene ratings, published 25 June 2026, https://www.gov.uk/food-hygiene-ratings : "Food businesses in Wales and Northern Ireland must display their hygiene rating sticker by law."

**10.4 [GUIDANCE, Scotland] Food Hygiene Information Scheme (FHIS).**
Status: VERIFIED. Sources: FSS, FHIS business guidance (no date shown), https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/business-guidance/running-a-food-business/hygiene-ratings-and-awards/food-hygiene-information-scheme-business-guidance ; FSS consumer page, https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/consumer-advice/food-safety/eating-out-of-home/food-hygiene-information-scheme-consumer-info

These requirements include the conditions found and the management procedures in place for providing safe food." Display: "Some businesses may choose not to display their rating on their website or premises, but all FHIS ratings are displayed on our website."

**10.5 Mandatory display in England: position at October 2026.**
Status: VERIFIED that it is a proposal only. No change in the law was found. Source: FSA, Future of Food Regulation report to the FSA Board: September 2026, GOV.UK, published 3 September 2026, updated 15 September 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/food-standards-agency-september-2026-board-meeting/future-of-food-regulation-report-to-the-fsa-board-september-2026

## 11. Traceability, supplier checks and food alerts

**11.1 [LAW, all nations] One step back.** Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 Article 18(2), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2002/178/article/18 : operators "shall be able to identify any person from whom they have been supplied with a food". And: "such operators shall have in place systems and procedures which allow for this information to be made available to the competent authorities on demand." VERIFIED.

**11.2 [LAW] One step forward applies to supplies to other businesses.** Article 18(3): operators "shall have in place systems and procedures to identify the other businesses to which their products have been supplied." VERIFIED. It refers to "other businesses", not to individual guests.

**11.3 [GUIDANCE, England, Wales, NI] What records to keep.**
Status: VERIFIED. Sources: FSA, Managing food safety (URL at 2.10); GOV.UK Running a food business, part "Suppliers and traceability".
Quotes: records should include "name and address of supplier", "type of and quantity of products" and "dates of transaction and of delivery". "Keep all records up to date and available for inspection at all times." And: "Assimilated Regulation (EU) 178/2002 (as amended) does not specify how long traceability records should be kept".
SFBB Suppliers and contractors: "Ideally, you should keep these records until you are sure that the food they refer to has been consumed without any problems."

**11.4 [GUIDANCE] Delivery checks.** GOV.UK Running a food business: "When food is delivered, check that:" "chilled and frozen food is cold enough", "the packaging is not damaged", "it is what you ordered". And: "If you think food has not been handled safely or is poor quality, do not use it." VERIFIED.

**11.5 [LAW, all nations] Withdrawal, recall and telling the authorities.** Article 19(1), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2002/178/article/19 : where an operator believes food is not safe, "it shall immediately initiate procedures to withdraw the food in question from the market" and "inform the competent authorities thereof." Article 19(3) is quoted at 12.1. VERIFIED.

**11.6 [GUIDANCE] How to subscribe to alerts.**
Status: VERIFIED.
- England, Wales, NI: GOV.UK, Sign up for food safety alerts, https://www.gov.uk/sign-up-for-food-safety-alerts : "If there is a problem with food on the market, the Food Standards Agency ( FSA ) will issue a food alert. - Scotland: FSS alerts at https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/alerts , with subscription settings at https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/alert-preferences ("Here you can manage the allergen alerts you receive or unsubscribe.").
- SFBB Product recall: "If you hear about a problem with a product, you should stop using/selling it straight away. You might also need to tell your customers."

**11.7 [GUIDANCE] A live example for caterers (10 October 2026).**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: FSA news story, GOV.UK, 10 October 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/news/the-food-standards-agency-calls-on-businesses-to-withdraw-and-recall-eggs-from-one-ukrainian-food-business-as-investigations-find-salmonella-enteritid

## 12. If guests report illness after an event

**12.1 [LAW, all nations] Tell the competent authority at once if your food may be harmful.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 Article 19(3).
Quote: "A food business operator shall immediately inform the competent authorities if it considers or has reason to believe that a food which it has placed on the market may be injurious to human health."
The same paragraph says operators "shall not prevent or discourage any person from cooperating" with the authorities.

**12.2 [GUIDANCE, England, Wales, NI] Who to tell and how.**
Status: VERIFIED. Sources: FSA, Food incidents, product withdrawals and recalls, GOV.UK, published 5 February 2018, last updated 20 August 2026, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/food-incidents-product-withdrawals-and-recalls ; FSA, Report a food safety incident for local authorities and businesses, GOV.UK, updated 5 August 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-a-food-problem/report-a-food-safety-incident
Quotes: "tell your competent authority (local authority or port health authority), which will advise you of any further action you might need to take". And: "We have removed the Incident Reporting Form and have replaced it with a simpler email registration process. From the 17 of November 2025 you should email the relevant team dealing with your incident". Addresses given on the page: FSA.Incidents@food.gov.uk (England), FSA.IncidentsWales@food.gov.uk (Wales), FSA.IncidentsNI@food.gov.uk (Northern Ireland).
For a single venue the first call is the council's environmental health team.

**12.3 [GUIDANCE] Guests report through the council too.** FSA, Report a food problem, GOV.UK, last updated 5 August 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-a-food-problem : members of the public can report "suspected food poisoning" to the local authority for the business. VERIFIED.

**12.4 [GUIDANCE, England and Wales] What the FSA tells the business to do with a complaint.**
Status: VERIFIED. Source: SFBB safe method "Customers" (URL at 2.8).
Quotes: "Always take complaints seriously, no matter what the circumstances". Steps: "Review the daily diary for details of any problems", "See if any other similar complaints have been received", "Check if any staff have reported illness in the days before". And: "While you are investigating, it may be appropriate to remove any of the same batch of food eaten from sale." And: "Record any changes in the relevant section of your pack and make a note in the diary."

**12.5 What to keep.** Status: partly VERIFIED. The law requires HACCP records and traceability records to exist and be available (2.3, 11.1). The Hornchurch case (4.2) shows the consequence of creating records after the event: the two individuals were jailed for perverting the course of justice. Records must be kept as they are; nothing should be completed or altered afterwards.

**12.6 Retained food samples.** Status: UNVERIFIED: no requirement or recommendation was found in the SFBB caterers' pack, Safe Catering or the CookSafe manual (text searches for "sample" and "retain"). Some caterers keep refrigerated samples of each dish for a few days after an event. Treat that as GOOD PRACTICE with no authoritative source found in this session.

**12.7 Insurer notification.** Status: UNVERIFIED (general insurance practice, not a food law point). Public and products liability policies normally require prompt notice of any event that may give rise to a claim, and may restrict admissions of liability. The venue should read its own policy condition and tell its broker early. No primary source was read for this.

**12.8 [GUIDANCE, Scotland]** FSS publishes Guidance on the management of outbreaks of foodborne illness in Scotland (https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/business-guidance/running-a-food-business/publications/guidance-on-the-management-of-outbreaks-of-foodborne-illness-in-scotland). It was located but not read. UNVERIFIED as to content.

## 15. Common myths the sources contradict

1. **"The law says cook to 75°C."** No UK nation sets a statutory cooking temperature. 70°C for 2 minutes and its equivalents are FSA "standard advice" (3.16). The only statutory heating figure is Scotland's 82°C for reheating (3.10).
2. **"The four-hour rule can be used more than once."** "you can only do this once" (5.2). The legal defence fails if the food was previously displayed above 8°C (3.2).
3. **"Hot food gets four hours too."** Hot food gets one period of less than two hours (3.5).
4. **"Top up the buffet as it empties."** "Make sure that food on display is used up before you add new food." (5.4)
5. **"Ninety minutes to cool is the law."** The law says "as quickly as possible" (3.12). No 90-minute figure appears in SFBB, Safe Catering or CookSafe (3.23).
6. **"Scotland has the same 8°C, four-hour and two-hour rules."** It does not (3.7 to 3.10, 3.15).
7. **"Staff must have a food hygiene certificate."** "Food handlers do not have to hold a food hygiene certificate." (9.7) The duty is supervision and instruction or training.
8. **"Forty-eight hours is the law."** It is guidance: "normally for 48 hours" (9.4). The statute sets no period.
9. **"A rating sticker has to be displayed everywhere."** Law in Wales and Northern Ireland only. Voluntary in England and Scotland (10.3, 10.4). England is under proposal, not changed (10.5).
10. **"Registration is a one-off form."** The duty to notify "any significant change in activities" is continuing (1.2).
11. **"Good-quality beef makes a pink burger safe."** "The quality of the meat you buy doesn't make any difference to the potential risk of contamination." (6.8)
12. **"Reheating rice makes it safe."** Toxins from rice left at room temperature: "Reheating will not get rid of these" (4.1).
13. **"British Lion eggs carry no risk."** "very low risk"; contamination "cannot be excluded" (8.2).
14. **"If we use an outside caterer it is nothing to do with us."** Each operator answers for the business under its control, and a venue that sells the food is placing it on the market (7.1). The defence is due diligence, which needs evidence (7.2).
15. **"The Hornchurch chef and manager were jailed for the food poisoning."** They were jailed for perverting the course of justice by falsifying records. The food safety conviction and the £1.5m fine were the company's (4.2).
16. **"Charity or one-off events are exempt."** "if an activity occurs only once or only low risk food is handled, it does not necessarily mean it is exempt from registration." (1.11)
