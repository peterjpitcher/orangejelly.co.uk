It is not approved for use in copy: only the fact banks in the two specs are, and some claims here were left out of them on purpose. See `../README.md`, section 2.3. Prepared 13 October 2026 as the evidence base for a 500 to 550 word printed article for BII members and a 2,000 word plus web guide. Every source was opened on 13 October 2026 unless stated.

## How to use this dossier

- Each fact has a reference number, a named source, a URL, a date, an exact quote of under 25 words and a status.
- **VERIFIED**: seen at the original source (the publisher's, vendor's or regulator's own page).
- **SECONDARY**: seen only in a press report or third-party page. Go to the original before printing.
- **UNVERIFIED**: a lead from search results or a blocked page. Do not print.
- **VENDOR CLAIM**: the vendor's own description or figure for its own product. It may be VERIFIED as something the vendor said, and still not be independent evidence that it is true.
- **DERIVED**: a figure worked out from published numbers (one case only, fact 4.1.2), with the sum shown.
- **NOT FOUND**: looked for and nothing located. This is not evidence that the thing does not exist.
- "Analysis" paragraphs are the researcher's reading of the evidence and are labelled as such.
- A printed magazine cannot be corrected. For print, use only VERIFIED facts, name the source in the sentence, and re-check any price on the day.

## Ten headline findings

1. **By the official measure, most hospitality businesses do not use AI.** 70.7% of accommodation and food service businesses told the ONS in late September 2026 that they do not currently use any AI technology; about 18% do (a derived figure: see fact 4.1.2). Those that do mostly use free tools, on a limited basis (facts 4.1.2, 4.1.4, 4.1.5).
2. **Customers are ahead of operators.** 19% of British consumers already use AI tools to plan a visit to a pub, bar or restaurant, mainly to check reviews, prices, menus and opening times (fact 4.2.1).
3. **A table can now be booked from inside an assistant in the UK.** Google's AI Mode since 10 April 2026, through eight named booking systems; ChatGPT since 10 August 2026, through OpenTable (facts 3.2.1, 3.2.2).
4. **Assistants now do jobs, not only answer questions.** ChatGPT Work (July 2026) and Claude Cowork (2026) take on multi-step tasks, use email and calendar connections and run scheduled tasks. Free tiers now include image creation, voice and research (section 1).
5. **AI is arriving inside systems pubs already pay for**, most visibly in accounts packages (Sage Copilot in every Sage Accounting plan, JAX in Xero at no extra charge, Intuit Intelligence in QuickBooks) and in tills (Square AI free in the UK since February 2026, Lightspeed AI, Toast IQ) (section 2).
6. **Several familiar names have no AI feature on their public sites**, including Zonal's EPoS, Dojo, RotaCloud and Erudus. Cellar and dispense "smart" kit is not AI and its makers do not call it AI (sections 2.9, 2.13).
7. **The BII already has a member case study and free training.** A Stonegate lessee in Ruislip using a £20 a month ChatGPT plan (October 2025), and the "AI for Publicans" video course and content calendar tool produced by Star Pubs (March 2026) (example 5.1, facts 7.5.1, 7.5.2).
8. **Getting recommended by AI is mostly about basics.** Google says there are "no additional requirements" for its AI answers and points to an up-to-date Business Profile; more reviews and positive ratings help local ranking; ChatGPT can only read a website that lets its crawler in (section 3.3).
9. **Four red lines are backed by official sources**: allergen information, legal and HR advice, pricing claims and fake reviews (section 6.11, section 8.6).
10. **New risks arrived this year**: AI-doctored complaint photos, AI-written staff grievances, adverts in free ChatGPT in the UK, and a customer backlash against AI artwork that made BBC News (facts 8.2.1, 8.2.2, 7.1.3, examples 5.17, 5.18).

## Method and limits (read before relying on anything)

- Sources were read directly: official statistics from the ONS spreadsheet, vendor and regulator pages from their own sites, trade bodies by scanning each site's full page list.
- Quotes were copied from the page text. Five helper researchers gathered 240 facts; 207 of their quotes were machine-checked against saved copies of the source pages. The rest (sites that block automated reading and had to be opened in a browser, or leads with no quote) are marked. The lead researcher's own quotes from browser-only pages (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, the FSB, Toast, Sage, QuickBooks, OpenTable and Parliament) were each re-checked against the live page on 13 October 2026.
- **The session's web search allowance ran out part-way through.** After that, only known addresses could be opened. Section 10 lists what this left uncovered. Nothing was estimated to fill a gap.
- Vendor pages change without notice and several carried introductory offers. Dates shown are the page's own date where it had one, otherwise "checked 13 October 2026".
- Some vendor websites carry text written for AI assistants (pre-written prompts and "for AI agents" links, seen on Nory, Bizimply, Vita Mojo and PolyAI among others). These were treated as page content and ignored.

## 1. What is new in the mainstream assistants (October 2025 to October 2026)

The single biggest change in twelve months: the assistants have moved from answering questions to doing jobs. All four main products now offer some version of an "agent" that can work through a multi-step task, use connected apps such as email and calendars, and produce a finished file. The facts below come from each vendor's own release notes, read on 13 October 2026. They describe what the vendor says the product does, not independent tests.

Important caveat for UK readers: several new features launched outside the UK first. Where the vendor's notes say so, it is recorded.

### 1.6 What this means in plain terms (analysis, not sourced fact)

- A landlord on a £18 to £20 a month plan can now hand over a whole job ("turn last month's till export into a one-page summary and a rota suggestion") instead of asking one question at a time. The vendors say the assistant asks before taking important actions. None of the four vendors promises the output is correct.
- Free tiers have become usable for real work: ChatGPT's free plan includes the newest model, limited images, voice and three scheduled tasks; Gemini's free plan includes images, Deep Research and voice. The ONS finding that hospitality mostly uses free tools (fact 4.1.4) fits this.
- Free and Go users of ChatGPT in the UK now see adverts, and individual plans may use chats for training unless the user opts out (facts 7.1.2 and 7.1.3). That matters for anything confidential.

## 2. AI now built into systems pubs already pay for

How to read this section. Every capability below is **the vendor's own description of its own product** (VENDOR CLAIM), taken from the vendor's own website on 13 October 2026 unless a different source is named. None has been independently tested. The full evidence for each line (exact quote, URL, date, notes) is in the annex (section 11), under the reference shown in brackets, for example [A1.6]. Prices are quoted in pounds only where the vendor publishes pounds.

Three patterns recur across almost every vendor:

1. **"Ask your data a question."** A chat box inside the system that answers plain-English questions about sales, labour or stock (Access Copilot, Lightspeed AI, Square AI, Toast IQ, Deputy AI, Sona's Raffy, Tenzo).
2. **Forecasts feeding rotas and orders.** Sales forecasts used to suggest staffing and purchasing (Fourth, Rotaready Evo, Nory, Tenzo, Procure Wizard, MarketMan, Apicbase).
3. **Answering the phone and the inbox.** AI that takes a booking call or drafts a reply (Collins Call Evo, SevenRooms Voice AI, OpenTable's voice partners, ResDiary Evo review replies).

And one honest finding: several well-known names publish **no AI feature at all** on their own sites. That is recorded too.

### 3.4 Practical checklist that follows from the sourced facts (analysis)

1. Keep the Google Business Profile complete and current: hours (including bank holidays), menu link, booking link, photos. Sourced to facts 3.3.1 and 3.3.2.
2. Ask for genuine reviews and reply to them. More reviews and positive ratings help local ranking (fact 3.3.2), and 45% of AI users are looking for reviews and ratings (fact 4.2.1). Never write or buy reviews (fact 8.6.1).
3. Put the menu, prices and opening times on the website as real text, not only as an image or PDF. Sourced to fact 3.3.1 ("important content is available in textual form").
4. Check the website is not blocking AI crawlers (fact 3.3.3).
5. If you take table bookings, being on a booking system that Google or OpenAI names is now the route into AI booking (facts 3.2.1 and 3.2.2).
6. Structured data is not a magic key. Google says no special markup is needed (fact 3.3.1). Any that exists must match what the page says.

### 4.5 Why the numbers differ so much

The ONS asks a weighted sample of all businesses which named technologies they use. The FSB and BCC survey their own members. Toast and Access survey people on their own lists or panels. A landlord who has asked ChatGPT to write a quiz would probably say "yes" to Toast's question and might say "no" or "not sure" to the ONS's list of technologies (note the 11.4% "not sure" in hospitality, almost double the 6.2% for all businesses). The honest summary for print is: by the official measure, most hospitality businesses do not yet use AI, and those that do mostly use free tools on a limited basis.

## 6. Practical use cases for an independent pub

Status of this section: this is the researcher's synthesis, not sourced fact. Each use case points to the evidence elsewhere in the dossier that shows somebody actually doing it, or a vendor actually selling it. Where no evidence was found, it says so. "General assistant" means ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot (see section 7 for prices).

Effort scale used below:
- **Minutes**: a free or £20 a month assistant, no set-up, can be tried today.
- **An afternoon**: needs your own information gathered first (menus, costs, house style) or a connection set up.
- **Supplier project**: depends on a system you buy or already pay for; ask the supplier.

Risk scale: **Low** (worst case is a bland post), **Medium** (could mislead a customer or cost money if unchecked), **High** (legal duty, safety, or someone's job).

### 6.11 The four red flags, with their sources

1. **Allergen information.** Legal duty on the food business (FSA, fact 8.6.2). The FSA's Science Council says "Food business operators remain responsible for ensuring food safety and regulatory compliance" (fact 8.6.2a). A software vendor in this field tells customers "do not use AI for any information that your business is required by law to provide to its customers" (annex A7.3). Never ask a general assistant what allergens are in a dish.
2. **Legal and HR advice.** The FSB's legal service provider, Markel, warns: "AI can often produce answers that sound entirely plausible but are actually incomplete, misleading, or simply incorrect." (fact 8.5.3). Acas: "these tools are not perfect and should not be solely relied on" (fact 8.6.1a). Staff are also using AI to write grievances (fact 8.2.2).
3. **Pricing claims and AI pricing.** Diners in one vendor survey reacted badly to AI-set prices (fact 4.3.3). Hidden or "dripped" fees were banned alongside fake reviews in April 2025 (fact 8.6.1). Any price, offer or claim an AI writes must be true and must be the price charged.
4. **Fake reviews.** Banned since 6 April 2025 (fact 8.6.1). Includes asking staff to write them and reviews "generated by software applications (such as bots)". Replying to real reviews with AI help is fine; inventing reviews is not.

## 10. Gaps, conflicts and uncertainties

Stated plainly, so nobody mistakes silence for a finding.

### 10.1 The search allowance ran out

The research session had a fixed allowance of web searches shared between the lead researcher and five helpers, and it ran out part-way through. After that, only pages at known addresses could be opened. The effects:

- **Not searched at all**: the latest news on Microsoft Copilot features; Ofcom figures on UK adults' use of AI; Apple and Meta AI features in the UK; ICO guidance for small businesses on generative AI; ASA or CAP guidance on AI-made images in advertising; AI energy schemes run by pub companies; independent coverage of vendor results; several pub companies (BrewDog, Fuller's, Shepherd Neame, Loungers, Oakman, Stonegate) and UK AI phone-answering start-ups.
- **Checked only on the body's own website**: the BII, UKHospitality, the BBPA, the Institute of Hospitality and the FSB. Members-only areas were not visible.
- A further search pass would close most of these gaps.

### 10.2 Statistics that could not be found

- **No pub-only AI adoption figure exists in anything found.** The ONS figure covers accommodation and food service as a whole (hotels, restaurants, takeaways, caterers and pubs together).
- **Nothing was found** from Barclays, Lloyds, Sage (as a survey), Deloitte, KPMG, Lumina Intelligence, Fourth (as a survey), UKHospitality, the BBPA or the BII giving a UK hospitality AI adoption percentage. For Barclays, Lloyds, Deloitte, KPMG, Lumina and Fourth one search each was run and returned nothing relevant; this is not proof that no such figure exists.
- **ONS confidence intervals** were not extracted. The ONS publishes them in a separate dataset. The hospitality figure rests on 913 responses, so treat single-point differences between waves as noise.

### 10.3 Weaknesses in the statistics that were found

- **Zonal and NIQ GO Technology report (19%)**: the full report is behind a download form and was not opened. Fieldwork dates and the exact question wording are unknown. The Gen Z and London figures were seen only in Restaurant Online.
