# Article 2 spec: food compliance and allergen handling at parties and events

**For:** BII News · **Print:** 300 words at most · **Guide:** `/guides/pub-event-food-safety-allergens` · **Printed link:** Peter's short link, `l.the-anchor.pub/<code>` (README section 6.1) · **Checked by:** the BII, before it prints (section 5.4)

Read `README.md` in this folder first. Its rules are part of this spec, and its section numbers are used below.

This is a compliance piece. People are harmed by allergen mistakes and pubs are prosecuted for them. Nearly every sentence you write here is a statement about the law or about official advice, so most of the wording is fixed for you. Your job is to assemble it accurately, not to improve it.

---

## 1. The brief

**Reader.** A licensee who takes party bookings: birthdays, christenings, wakes, weddings, work parties, buffets, set-menu functions.

**Why this piece.** The BII and the pub trade press have published nothing on allergen handling for pre-booked parties, buffets or pre-orders. Three years of BII Christmas guides leave it out. This fills a real gap.

**The angle.** A party is not normal service. One person orders for many people, often weeks ahead and by email. The food goes on a buffet or out in a rush. Extra hands join the shift. A kitchen that copes well every day can still be caught out, and a pub can follow every rule and still serve the wrong plate. The piece gives the checks that close those gaps, and says plainly which are law and which are advice.

**The one thing the reader should do.** Scan the code for the booking questions, the team briefing and the event-day checklist.

**What it must not do.**

- It must not turn advice into law, or law into advice.
- It must not give one rule for the whole UK where Scotland differs.
- It must not tell a scare story. One case is used in the guide, with care. None is used in print.
- It must not repeat the existing guide `/guides/food-allergies-gdpr-compliance`, which covers the everyday allergen system and data protection. This guide is about events and links to that one.

## 5. The printed article

### 5.1 The plan

Seven numbered checks. Each one opens with a status label in bold, **The law:** or **FSA advice:**, so the reader can see at a glance which is which. That label is the device that keeps this piece accurate in 300 words: do not remove it and do not swap one for the other.

The table gives every part in order. "Locked" wording is used exactly as written. Where it says "yours", you write it, inside the word budget and the rules.

| Part | Wording | Words |

## 9. Not settled: say this and nothing more

The research found no official answer on these four points. Use the wording given. Do not offer an opinion and do not fill the gap.

1. **Whether a party pre-order counts as distance selling.** Write: "The rule for food ordered by phone or online is clear. 2. **Who is responsible when the customer hires an outside caterer.** Write: "A caterer supplying food to your guests is a food business in its own right and answers for its own food. No regulator has published a statement on how responsibility divides between the venue and a caterer the customer brings in, so ask your council's food safety team before the event, and remember that whatever your own staff store, reheat or serve is your responsibility."
3. **Food that guests bring themselves, such as a celebration cake.** Write: "No regulator has published a rule on who holds the allergen duty for food a customer brings in. 4. **Whether buffets, barbecues, hog roasts or outside catering are a "significant change" you must tell the council about.** Write: "The law does not list what counts as a significant change, and no official source names buffets, barbecues, hog roasts or outside catering.
