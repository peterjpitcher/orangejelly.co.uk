# orangejelly.co.uk language review, read as a hospitality owner

Date: 2 September 2026
Reviewed: every non-blog page on the live site (26 pages), plus header and footer.
Lens: a pub or restaurant owner who has never bought consultancy, reads on a phone between shifts, and wants to know three things: what do you do, will it work for me, what does it cost.

The short version: the writing is confident, honest and often genuinely good. The problem is that it is written in the language of a strategy consultancy, and a licensee has to translate it. The hospitality page is the easiest page to read on the site, and it is the one you cannot reach from the menu.

---

## Site-wide fixes (do these first, they touch every page)

### 1. Lead with the plain label, not the consultancy label
The six "places growth gets stuck" are labelled Create demand, Convert more, Protect margin, Remove operational drag, Improve the experience, Build for scale. Every one of them already has a plain one-liner underneath that a publican would recognise instantly. Swap them round so the plain line is the headline.

| Current label | What a licensee would say |
|---|---|
| Create demand | Not enough people know about you |
| Convert more | People look, but don't book |
| Protect margin | Busy, but not making money |
| Remove operational drag | Too much admin, not enough time |
| Improve the experience | Customers don't come back |
| Build for scale | It only works because you're there |

Then the six-word tags can sit underneath as the category, if you still want them.

### 2. Decide whether it is six or eight
Home, Solutions and the Growth Pressure Map say six. The Growth Problems page and nav say eight. The professional services page says six. Fractional CMO says "the eight growth problems". As a reader I assumed I had missed two. Either call the two extras (Growth has stalled, Using AI intelligently) what they are, which is combinations of the six, or make everything say eight.

### 3. One story on price
Three pages say three different things:
- Start here: "the rate is £62.50 plus VAT an hour, and that is the whole price list."
- Pub marketing FAQ: "There is no price list, because there is no package."
- Why revenue is falling FAQ: "There is no price on this site."

An owner who reads two of those will not trust either. Put the hourly rate and the "hours quoted in writing before we start" line in one sentence and use that same sentence everywhere the question comes up, including the home page.

### 4. Retire the recurring jargon
These words appear on most pages and none of them are pub words:

| Word on the site | Say instead |
|---|---|
| baseline | where you started / the numbers before we began |
| run rate | what you were doing before |
| conversion journey / the journey | the steps from someone being interested to actually booking |
| proposition | what you offer and why anyone should pick you |
| operational drag | admin and manual work |
| cost-to-serve | what it costs you to do each job |
| engagement / diagnostic / scope | the work / the first look / agree what we'll do |
| artefact | document |
| leading and lagging indicators | early signs and final results |
| single points of failure | things that stop if one person is off |
| Search Console clicks and impressions | how often people found us on Google |
| strategic growth partner | (see point 5) |

### 5. "Strategic growth partner for ambitious small and mid-sized businesses"
This appears in the home hero, the About page, the Pub marketing hero, the footer and the meta description. To a pub owner it says "expensive and not for me". The 10 to 500 people guide reinforces it (most pubs are 5 to 30). Keep the ambition, lose the category: "We find what's stopping your business growing, then fix it." On the pub page, say "We run a pub. We help other pubs grow" and nothing more.

### 6. Fewer apologies, shorter
The site spends a lot of words on what it will not do, does not have and cannot prove: "who this doesn't work for", "what we normally decline", "what we will not do", "what we do not have", "what this will not tell you", and three problem pages that open their proof section with "we have no number". The honesty is the best thing about the brand. It just needs to be one crisp sentence each time, not a paragraph. An owner reads a long "we can't prove this" section as "they can't do this".

### 7. Stop leaning on the method names
HEAR, CHALLENGE, BUILD, OPTIMISE appear as section headings on the results pages, as a line on every problem page ("This is the HEAR and CHALLENGE half of the method"), and as the H1 on the home page section and How we work. Unless someone has read How we work first, those four words carry no meaning. Use plain headings and let the method be the thing you explain once.

### 8. Repetition of The Anchor line
"The Anchor, our own venue" plus a version of "where getting it wrong cost us rather than a client" appears on 20 of the 26 pages, sometimes twice on one page. Once per page is plenty, and on the problem pages it can go entirely because the results link covers it.

### 9. Sentence-fragment headlines
Several H1s are half sentences: "for business owners ready to take control of growth." and "the problem decides the tool." The lowercase-with-full-stop style is a brand choice and it works when the line is a full thought. When it is a fragment, the first thing an owner reads is a sentence that doesn't start.

### 10. Navigation
- "Unlock growth" as a menu label means nothing. "Growth problems" or "What's stuck?" does.
- There is no menu link to Pub marketing. A licensee arrives, sees "ambitious small and mid-sized businesses" and leaves. Put "Pubs" or "Hospitality" in the main menu.
- Three ways in (Let's talk, Start here, Contact) with the same form. Fine, but the footer group called "Start" then listing Start here, Unlock growth, How we work, Results reads like a to-do list.

## Page by page

### Home (/)
What is hard:
- Hero: "strategic growth partner for ambitious businesses" then "for business owners ready to take control of growth." Two fragments, one category word. The second paragraph is much better ("Sometimes that's marketing. Often it isn't.").
- "Any one of those is worth an hour of our time and none of yours until it is." Needs two reads. Drop it or say "Any of those is worth an hour. It costs you nothing."
- "hear. challenge. build. optimise." as a section heading, followed by "The order is the method." Meaningless without context.
- Results tiles: each ends with a category tag (Create demand, Protect margin) that reads as part of the sentence.
- "A pub is not a professional services firm. Demand, conversion, margin and drag behave the same way in both, which is why the method travels and the tactics do not." Four abstract nouns and a metaphor.
- "Roughly 10 to 500 people" excludes most pubs on the one page they are most likely to land on.

Suggested rewrites:
- Hero: "You're working harder than ever and the business isn't growing. We find out why, then fix it. Sometimes that's marketing. Often it isn't."
- Method heading: "How it works: listen, check the numbers, fix it, measure it."
- Travel line: "We proved this in a pub. The same problems, and the same fixes, show up in every business."
- Add a short line under the results: "Run a pub? Start here." linking to /pub-marketing.

### Start here (/start-here)
This is the most important page on the site and it is nearly there. It is long, though.
What is hard:
- "before anyone says the word solution" is fine. "WE SCOPE IT" is not; say "We agree the work."
- "A defined piece of work, a fixed fee agreed before anything starts" and later "the rate is £62.50 plus VAT an hour" read as a contradiction. Say "a fixed quote, based on how many hours we think it needs, in writing before we start."
- "We cannot find a margin problem in a business that will only show us its revenue." A licensee would say "we need to see the real takings and costs, not just the sales figure."
- "A diagnostic is normally two to three weeks." Say "The first proper look normally takes two to three weeks."
- The "who this doesn't work for" list is six items. Cut to three or four and make each one line.
- Form: "Work email". Most owners will type a Gmail address. Just "Email".
- FAQ "Do you only work with hospitality?" answer contains "The problems it solves are not hospitality-shaped". Say "The same problems show up in every business."

### What we build (/solutions)
What is hard:
- H1 "the problem decides the tool." reads as a fragment and "tool" is odd for a service.
- "Orange Jelly builds fixes, not services." Clever, but an owner wonders what they are actually buying.
- The 13-item list mixes plain items (Websites and booking systems) with trade terms: Brand and proposition, CRM and communications, Procurement and supplier terms, Team standards, playbooks and SOPs, AI use cases and prototypes.
- "what we normally decline" is a fifth list of things you will not do.

Suggested rewrites:
- H1: "What we actually do depends on what's wrong."
- Brand and proposition: "What you offer, and how you describe it."
- CRM and communications: "Staying in touch with customers who've already been."
- Procurement and supplier terms: "Supplier costs and contracts."
- Team standards, playbooks and SOPs: "Ways of working the team can follow when you're not there."
- AI use cases and prototypes: "Where AI would genuinely help, and where it wouldn't."
- Cut the decline list to two lines under the "it always starts the same way" section.

### How we work (/how-we-work)
The densest page on the site. A pub owner will skim it.
What is hard:
- "why a method, and not just experience." Then a paragraph about credibility. The owner does not care why you have a method; they want to know what happens.
- "Understand what is really happening, not what the brief says." Owners do not write briefs.
- "Root cause, funnel, margin, process, journey, systems and data, then sizing and prioritising what is actually worth doing." Seven abstract nouns in a row.
- "A Growth Pressure Map" is a product name for a document. Say "a one-page map of where the pressure is in your business."
- "smallest intervention capable of making a material difference", "prototype early", "operating rhythm", "adoption problems", "leading indicators as well as the lagging ones", "learning log", "impact report".
- "what this stops happening." Awkward heading. "What this avoids."
- The nine-item "what we agree before anything is built" list is good and plain. Keep it.

Suggested structure: four steps, each with one plain sentence on what happens and one on what you get. Example for step one: "We talk to you and the team, walk through how things work today and look at the real numbers. You get a plain statement of the problem and the figures we'll measure against."

### Results (/results)
Mostly clear. Two fixes:
- Category tags after each number read as run-on text.
- "why they're all one business." is a good, honest section but it spends four paragraphs on it. Two would do.
- "Search Console clicks and impressions" appears under the headline number on every page. Say "how often people found us on Google."

### Results detail pages (three of them)
- HEAR / CHALLENGE / BUILD / OPTIMISE as headings. Replace with "What was happening / What we found / What we did / What changed".
- "why this travels." Replace with "Why this applies to you, whatever you run."
- The content under the headings is the best-written material on the site. Leave it alone.
- "Nobody could find us": "a room for forty, somewhere that would do a wake, a Sunday roast that was still on at three" is exactly right. More of this.

### Growth problems index (/growth-problems)
- Title "Unlock growth: eight places it gets stuck". "Unlock growth" is a nav label, not a sentence.
- The eight one-liners are good. "Manual effort is absorbing the capacity growth needs" is the exception: say "Admin is eating the hours you'd need to grow."
- "falling rather than flat?" section is a good idea. Simplify: "Is revenue actually going down? That's more urgent. Start here instead."

### Growth problem pages (eight of them)
Same template on all eight, so the same fixes apply to all:
- "the connected causes" heat map labels each area "critical" or "steady". "Steady" reads as either "fine" or "stuck". Say "usually part of it" and "usually not".
- "This is the HEAR and CHALLENGE half of the method. The build comes after the evidence, not before it." Delete, or say "We find the cause first. The fix comes after."
- The "sound familiar?" lists are the best part of each page. Plain, short, recognisable.
- Page-specific:
  - Growth has stalled: proof paragraph packs all five numbers and a caveat into one block. Split it or use the tiles.
  - Weak demand: "channel dependence", "the proposition, tested", "what compounds". Say "you rely on one or two ways of getting customers", "can people tell what you're for?", "what keeps working after you stop pushing".
  - Leads not converting: good. "Price objections arrive before value was ever shown" could be "People ask the price before they know what they'd get."
  - Margin under pressure: "cost-to-serve", "price and mix, against value". Say "what each job costs you to do", "what you charge, and what sells, against what people would pay". The "Now be exact about what that number is" paragraph is honest but long; two sentences would do.
  - Operations slowing us down: the whole proof section is a 150-word explanation of why there is no number. Cut to: "We don't have a measured result for this one yet, and we won't borrow one. Here's what we'd look at instead."
  - Experience leaking value: same, "Straight answer first: we have no retention number" then five sentences on it. Two sentences.
  - Using AI intelligently: "AI-shaped confusion", "repetitive judgement", "build-versus-buy line". Say "where the same decisions get made over and over", "what to buy, what to build, what to leave alone".
  - Systems can't keep up: "Growth is being rationed by spreadsheets, workarounds and heroics." Good line. "the data foundation" and "sequence of the fix" are not. Say "do your numbers live in one place?" and "what to fix first".

### About (/about)
Warm and human. Small fixes:
- Opening line repeats "strategic growth partner for ambitious small and mid-sized businesses".
- "That range is only defensible because of the method." Say "We can only do all of that because we always work the same way."
- "we run a business, not just a practice." "Practice" is a consultancy word. "We run a pub, not just a consultancy."
- "the shortest way to find out." Good.
- The Facts block is excellent. Keep it.

### Contact (/contact)
Fine. "Work email" to "Email". The line about the single mailbox is good.

### Pub marketing (/pub-marketing)
The clearest page on the site and the one a licensee should land on.
- Hero: drop the "strategic growth partner for ambitious small and mid-sized businesses" opener. "We run a pub. This is what we'd look at in yours."
- "What we look at first" is exactly the right register. "Whether the booking survives the interest" is the only line that needs a second read: "Whether people who want to come actually manage to book."
- Cost FAQ contradicts Start here (see site-wide fix 3).
- "where we can get to in person" is good and practical. Keep.
- "we're a tenant too." Good. Keep.
- The method section reuses the four capital words; "How it works" with plain labels would be enough.

### Why revenue is falling (/why-revenue-is-falling)
Strong page, right tone for someone worried. Fixes:
- Kicker "a direction, not a verdict" means nothing.
- "BDM" is not spelled out. Say "your pubco business development manager".
- "a margin problem wearing a demand problem's clothes" and "paid for twice: once when you held it, once when you filled it with nothing" are vivid but each needs a second read. One is fine; two in a row is a pattern.
- "It's not fast in the way a promise is fast. It's fast in the way that stops you paying for the wrong fix." Say "It's not instant. It does stop you paying for the wrong fix."
- FAQ answers run to 150 words each. Halve them.

### Professional services (/sectors/professional-services)
Written in that audience's language on purpose (realisation, utilisation, charge-out rates), so leave the body alone. Two notes:
- H1 "the growth problem is rarely the one on the agenda." is a fragment.
- The "what we do not have" section is again a list of absences. Fine here because that audience will ask, but keep it to three lines.

### AI readiness (/tools/ai-readiness)
- "No score" is said three times before the questions start. Once.
- Two statements are reverse-scored ("The same information gets typed into more than one system", "Work stalls because it's waiting on one particular person"). Mixed with the positive ones, the Never/Always scale is confusing to answer. Either reword them so "Always" is always good, or add a one-line note.
- The six-area explanations underneath are clear. Good page otherwise.

### Insights (/insights)
- "Every piece ends at the problem underneath it rather than at a contact form." Clever, but a second-read line. "No sales pitch at the end."
- The hospitality redirect line is helpful. Keep.

### Guides (/guides)
- "playbooks" is trade jargon. "Guides" already works; "Summer pub guides" is enough.
- "There are 105 of them" in the intro versus "100+" in the title. Pick one.
- "The thinking underneath is the same commercial thinking we bring to any ambitious small business." Cut; a licensee on this page does not need the repositioning explained.
- Otherwise the most licensee-friendly copy on the site.

### Fractional CMO (/fractional-cmo)
Not a hospitality page, and the audience will follow it. Minor: "the wrong shape of answer", "remit", "functional leaders" are corporate. The five questions at the end are excellent and plain.

### Header and footer
- Menu: "Unlock growth" to "Growth problems". Add "Pubs".
- Footer tagline "AI is part of the toolkit, not the product." Fine.
- Footer: "A strategic growth partner for ambitious small and mid-sized businesses." Same fix as everywhere.

## Suggested order of work
1. Site-wide fixes 1 to 5 (labels, six vs eight, price, jargon, the "strategic growth partner" line). These are copy edits in shared components and constants.
2. Home and Start here hero rewrites.
3. Problem-page template: heat-map labels, method line, trim the "no number" sections.
4. How we work rewrite.
5. Everything else is polish.
