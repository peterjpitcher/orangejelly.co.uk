# What we need from the design team

Plain English. Written so Peter can forward it as it stands.

---

## The one thing we actually need

**A white version of the logo, in the wide shape.**

The orange header needs a light logo. At the moment we take the dark one and flip it
to white with a colour filter. On screen you cannot tell the difference. But it is a
trick rather than a real file, and it will come out wrong the moment anyone prints a
page or drops the logo into a document.

Same wide lockup you already sent, in white. That is the whole ask.

---

## Three things to tell them, no action needed unless they disagree

**1. We darkened three of their colours slightly.**

Each one was too pale to read against the background it sits on. They already agreed
to two of them. The third is the grey used for small print: hints under form fields,
breadcrumbs, the caption under a card. It went from `#757784` to `#666873`.

Nothing looks different. The text is just readable now. Peter has approved this.

**2. Orange sections now use a deeper orange.**

Peter asked for white text on orange backgrounds, for impact. On the brand orange,
white text is not readable, so those sections use a darker shade of the same orange
where it is. Buttons and tags keep the original orange.

**3. Buttons changed, and it is more than the outline.**

Peter asked for a white outline on buttons over orange, and for white text on any
orange button. The second one forced the first to go further than expected: white text
cannot sit on their orange at all, it is not readable, so an orange button is now a
darker shade of the same orange.

The white outline is on every dark background, not only the orange ones. It is not on
the cream pages, where a white outline would simply disappear and take the heavy dark
outline of the design with it. The rule is that the outline is whichever of black or
white stands out most against what is behind it.

Two buttons were invisible before this and nobody had noticed: a dark button on a dark
section, with a dark outline. They read as a line of text rather than a button.

---

## One question for them

**The photos on the guides page.**

Every guide card shows a photo with a word across the middle of it, like "Marketing"
or "Events". The word is white and sits on a dark tint laid over the photo.

We checked all 105 photos. On 80 of them the word is too faint to read reliably, and
on the worst ones it is barely visible at all. The tint is not strong enough.

Making the tint dark enough to fix it would need it roughly twice as strong, which
would hide most of the photo. So we have put the word on a small solid block instead.
It now reads the same on every card, and the photo stays visible.

If they would rather solve it another way, we will change it. We wanted them to know
the cards look slightly different, and why.
