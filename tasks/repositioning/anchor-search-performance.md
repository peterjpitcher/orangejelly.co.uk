# The Anchor: search performance, 12 months to 26 August 2026

Source: `data/gsc-the-anchor-2026-08-26/`, Google Search Console export, web search, last 12 months.

**This is the wrong property for the URL plan.** It is `the-anchor.pub`, not `orangejelly.co.uk`.
It does not tell us what the 106 Orange Jelly posts and 33 routes earn, which is the thing blocking
the redirect decision. That export is still needed.

It is, however, the single best piece of proof material anyone has produced for this project.

## The finding that matters

A pub earns **41.7% of its organic search clicks from aviation content**, not from pub content.

| Page group | Clicks | Share of all site clicks |
|---|---|---|
| Aviation and near-Heathrow pages | 6,103 | **41.7%** |
| `/blog/heathrow-plane-spotting-locations` alone | 3,729 | **25.5%** |
| All `/blog/*` (323 pages) | 6,499 | 44.4% |

One blog post about where to watch aeroplanes brings in a quarter of everything.

For context, `/blog/heathrow-plane-spotting-locations` earned 103,268 impressions on its own. The
homepage earned 69,274.

## Why this is the case study the repositioning needs

`08-proof-and-case-studies.md` asks every case study to answer "what made it transferable". This one
answers it better than any of the five approved percentage claims, and it does so without needing a
single number verified.

The story is not "we did good pub marketing". The story is:

> The business assumed its search opportunity was pub search. It was not. The real demand sat in an
> adjacent interest nobody inside the business would have named, and it was almost entirely
> unserved. We found it, built the definitive resource for it, and it now brings in more than four
> in ten of all search visits.

That transfers to an accountancy practice, a law firm, an electrical contractor or a manufacturer
without changing a word of the logic. It is a demand-discovery story, not a hospitality story, which
is precisely the reframing the whole repositioning depends on.

It also demonstrates the method end to end. HEAR: what is the business actually near. CHALLENGE: the
assumed search market is not the real one. BUILD: the resource. OPTIMISE: 6,103 clicks and still
growing.

**Recommendation: make this the lead case study on the Results page**, ahead of the booking and
revenue percentages. It needs no claim verification, it is independently checkable in Search
Console, and it argues for the new positioning rather than the old one.

## What it does not do

It does not verify the `+828% search visibility` claim in `/CLAIMS.md`. This export covers 12 months
with no baseline. Verifying that claim (decision D2) needs the earlier comparison period, the exact
definition used, and a stated data source. Still outstanding.

## Secondary observations

- **Mobile is 78% of clicks and ranks eight positions better than desktop.** Whatever the redesign
  does, mobile is the primary surface, not the responsive afterthought. The design system's mobile
  navigation work matters more than it looks.
- **Brand queries earn 1,746 clicks** in the top 998 queries against 1,401 for aviation queries, but
  the aviation *pages* earn far more than the aviation *queries* visible in the export. The long
  tail is doing the work: hundreds of low-volume variants, none individually significant.
  That is what a genuinely dominant content asset looks like, and it is an argument for depth over
  breadth in the Orange Jelly insight strategy.
- **323 of 624 pages are blog posts**, and they earn 44.4% of clicks. Content is the engine at The
  Anchor exactly as it is at Orange Jelly.
