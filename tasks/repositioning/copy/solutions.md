# `/solutions` copy

**Task:** T068, brought forward. **Built as:** `src/app/solutions/page.tsx`.

**Why it moved.** T068 sat in phase 5, but the phase 4 redirect table sends `/capabilities` here, so
phase 4 cannot ship without it. Building it now removes the blocker rather than discovering it on
release day.

**The argument the page has to make.** Thirteen capabilities is a list, and a list of capabilities reads
as a company that will do anything. The page therefore refuses to lead with them. It leads with the six
places growth gets stuck, puts the capabilities underneath as the things a fix might be made of, and says
plainly that nobody buys one of them on its own.

No prices (D3), no packages, and the entry is always a conversation (D8).

## What a fix can be made of

**Heading:** what a fix can be made of.

**Intro:** Thirteen things we build with. Nobody buys one of them on its own, and if somebody asks us for
one before we have understood the problem we will say so.

- **Growth and commercial strategy.** Where the business is going, and what it stops doing to get there.
- **Brand and proposition.** What you actually offer, in words a customer would use.
- **Marketing and demand.** Making enough of the right people know and care.
- **Conversion and customer journeys.** Closing the gap between interest and a decision.
- **Websites and booking systems.** The place the decision usually gets made, or lost.
- **CRM and communications.** Staying useful to people who have already bought.
- **Pricing and margin.** What is left after the sale, and why more of it is not.
- **Procurement and supplier terms.** The cost line nobody has looked at in three years.
- **Operations and workflows.** Removing the work that should not exist.
- **Data and dashboards.** The three numbers that would change a decision, not the ninety that will not.
- **Automation and internal tools.** Software instead of somebody's Tuesday.
- **AI use cases and prototypes.** Where it earns its place, and an honest answer where it does not.
- **Team standards, playbooks and SOPs.** Making the improvement survive the person who made it.

## What we normally decline

**Heading:** what we normally decline.

- Executing a plan that is already decided, where the value we would add is speed and nothing else.
- Any engagement where we cannot reach the people, the data or the customers.
- Work with no agreed measure of success, because there is then no honest way to end it.
- Technology chosen before the problem was defined, including ours.
- Anything we would be the second-best supplier for. There is usually somebody better and we would rather
  say who.

---

## How it starts

**Heading:** it always starts the same way.

Nobody buys a solution from this page. Every engagement starts with a conversation about what is actually
happening, and the first one is an hour and free. What gets built, and what it costs, is agreed after
that and in writing.

## Amendments, 2 September 2026: plain-English pass

The site was reviewed as a hospitality business owner would read it, and Peter approved a plain-English rewrite. The lines below are the wording now on the page and supersede the earlier versions above where they differ. Consultancy terms (baseline, run rate, proposition, conversion journey, operational drag, diagnostic) came out; the one price sentence is used everywhere cost comes up; the category terms stay as small labels and the headings say the problem in the reader's words.

- What you actually sell, in words a customer would use.
- Closing the gap between someone asking and someone buying.
- Staying useful to people who have already bought, so they come back.
- Removing the jobs that should not exist.
