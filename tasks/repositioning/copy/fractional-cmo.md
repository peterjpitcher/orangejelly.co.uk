**The argument this page has to make.** Four fractional terms all sit in the 500 tier, one at
competition index 12, and the brand pack does not mention the category once. It is real demand in
established language. The risk is equally real: fractional implies one functional seat, and Orange
Jelly is deliberately cross-functional.

So the page uses the language to be found and then argues against the format. A business with a
problem across demand, margin and operations does not need a fractional CMO. It needs somebody who
can see all three. That is a genuinely strong argument rather than a positioning dodge, and it is
honest about when a fractional CMO IS the right answer, because a page that says "never hire one"
would be as unbelievable as one that says "always".

## what a fractional cmo actually is.

A senior marketer, part time, on your leadership team. Usually one to three days a week, usually on a
rolling contract, usually somebody who has done the job full time somewhere larger.

It is a good model. It exists because a business can need senior marketing judgement long before it
can justify a full-time salary for it, and the market has correctly worked out that the gap is real.

## when it is the right answer.

Three cases, and they are common:

- **You know the problem is marketing.** Demand is genuinely the constraint, the rest of the business
  works, and what is missing is somebody senior to own it.
- **You have a team to lead.** There are people doing marketing already and the gap is direction,
  standards and someone accountable for the number.
- **The work is continuous.** Marketing is not a project for you, it is an operating function that
  needs running week after week.

If all three are true, hire one. We will say so on the call, and we can usually suggest where to look.

## when it is the wrong shape.

**The problem crosses functions and only one of them gets a seat.**

This is the common case and it is the reason this page exists. A business comes to the conclusion
that it needs marketing leadership because demand is the symptom it can see. Underneath, enquiries
are not converting because the handover is broken, margin is thin because pricing was never
revisited, and the team is doing by hand what a system should do.

A fractional CMO will diagnose that correctly and then be able to act on roughly a third of it. The
other two thirds are somebody else's remit, and the honest ones will tell you so. Now you are hiring
a fractional COO as well, and the two of them are negotiating the boundary between them at your
expense.

**Nobody is accountable for the whole number.** Split the problem across two part-time functional
leaders and you have split the accountability with it. Each can succeed at their remit while the
thing you actually wanted does not move.

**A seat is not a diagnosis.** Deciding you need a CMO is deciding what the answer is before anybody
has established what the question was. That is the same mistake as buying software before defining
the problem, and it is more expensive.

## what we do instead.

We are not a fractional anything and we do not want a seat on your leadership team.

We take the problem, work out what is actually causing it across every function it touches, and build
the fix. Sometimes that is marketing. Often it is a pricing change, a process, a system, or three
small things in three different places that only make sense together.

Then we leave. An engagement that has done its job should end, which is the other thing a monthly
retainer for a seat is not designed to do.

## the questions to ask either of us.

Ask these of a fractional CMO and of us. The answers will tell you which shape you need.

- What would you look at first, and why that?
- What would make you say this is not a marketing problem?
- What does success look like, as a number, and when would we know?
- What happens to this when you are not here any more?
- What would make you tell me to stop paying you?

**Close:** If the answers to the second and the fifth are vague, that is your answer, whoever you are
talking to.
