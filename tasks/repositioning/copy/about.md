## What we actually are

**Heading:** what we actually are.

We get under the skin of a business, work out what is genuinely blocking growth, and build the thing that
fixes it. The fix might be marketing. It might be a pricing change, a process, a system, an automation, or
a piece of software with some AI in it. The problem decides, and the problem is not usually what the brief
says it is.

That range is only defensible because of the method. Without it, a company that does marketing, commercial
change, operations and technology reads as a company that will do anything. Four steps, in the same order,
every time: hear, challenge, build, optimise.

## Where the thinking comes from

**Heading:** we run a business, not just a practice.

The Anchor is our own venue in Stanwell Moor, run as a Greene King tenancy since 2019. It is not a case
study we bought and it is not a client. It is a business that has to trade every week, with a wage bill, a
cellar, suppliers who put prices up and customers who go elsewhere when it is not good enough.

Everything on this site was built and tested there first. Search visibility grew 828%, table bookings
403%, private hire 567%, food revenue 98% in three months and no-shows fell 89%: all measured against a
baseline, all in a business where getting it wrong cost us rather than a client.

That is a different kind of experience from advising. It is the difference between knowing that a booking
which does not turn up is expensive and having stood in a kitchen at seven o'clock with the food already
prepped.

## What running one teaches you

**Heading:** what running one teaches you.

Three things keep showing up, in our own business and in everybody else's.

**The problem is almost never the one being described.** Flat trade was blamed on the economy for a long
time before anybody checked what people were actually searching for. Every business has an explanation it
has agreed on, and the explanation is usually the thing standing between it and the answer.

**The expensive failures are the quiet ones.** A booking that never gets made costs nothing anyone can
see. A dish that sells well and contributes little looks like success on the revenue line. Nobody puts
either on a report, which is exactly why they persist.

**Nothing changes because of a document.** Work is not finished when a deliverable is handed over. It is
finished when the change is running, the people using it understand it, and the number has moved or been
honestly reported as not having moved.

## What we will not do

**Heading:** what we will not do.

- **Sell you activity because it is easy to sell.** Three posts a week is a product. It is rarely an
  answer.
- **Put AI in something because AI is in the brief.** It is part of the toolkit. If the answer is a
  pricing change and a phone call, that is what you get.
- **Agree with you for money.** If we think the direction is wrong we will say so, with the reasoning,
  before the work rather than after it.
- **Keep going once the useful outcome has arrived.** An engagement that has done its job should end, and
  we would rather tell you that than let it drift into a retainer.
- **Take work we cannot do well.** Some of what comes to us is not for us, and saying so early is cheaper
  for everybody than finding out in month three.
