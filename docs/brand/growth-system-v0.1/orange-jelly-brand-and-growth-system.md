# 01. Company Vision

## One-sentence definition

**Orange Jelly is the growth partner for ambitious small and mid-sized businesses.**

It finds what is blocking progress and builds practical solutions using creativity, technology, AI and commercial judgement.

## The vision

**To help ambitious smaller businesses outthink and outperform much larger competitors.**

Large organisations can buy specialist teams, consultants, technology and transformation programmes. Smaller businesses are often expected to solve equally complex problems with fewer people, less time and less access to specialist thinking.

Orange Jelly exists to close that gap.

It gives ambitious businesses access to inventive, technology-enabled problem solving without forcing them into an enterprise consultancy model or a narrow agency retainer.

## The mission

**To expose what is blocking growth and make the right change happen.**

Orange Jelly gets close enough to the business to understand the real problem, challenges assumptions that are holding it back, and builds solutions that can be implemented and measured.

Those solutions might involve:

- Marketing and demand generation
- Sales and conversion
- Customer journeys and digital experiences
- Pricing, margin and commercial performance
- Operations, workflows and team processes
- Inventory, procurement and supplier spend
- Data, reporting and decision support
- Automation, software and practical AI

The solution is determined by the problem. Orange Jelly does not force every client through the same service catalogue.

## The purpose

**Give ambitious businesses an unfair advantage.**

The advantage does not always come from advanced technology. Sometimes it is a better decision, a simpler process, a stronger proposition or a clearer commercial focus.

AI is valuable when it can accelerate learning, improve conversion, remove operational drag, make better use of information or enable something that was previously unaffordable. It is not automatically the answer.

## The core belief

Growth is rarely blocked by one isolated issue.

A revenue problem may also be a conversion problem, a customer experience problem, a pricing problem, an operating model problem or a systems problem. Fixing only the visible symptom can create more activity without creating better results.

Orange Jelly looks at the whole growth system, finds the pressure points and focuses effort where it can make a material difference.

## The brand promise

> **You bring the growth problem. We build the solution.**

This promise is intentionally broad enough to cover different business functions, but specific about Orange Jelly's role. The client brings a meaningful commercial challenge. Orange Jelly brings diagnosis, challenge, design and delivery.

## What success looks like

Orange Jelly succeeds when:

- The client can clearly describe the problem being solved.
- Both parties agree what success looks like before substantial work begins.
- Orange Jelly has access to the people, information and operating reality needed to diagnose honestly.
- The proposed change is bold enough to matter and practical enough to implement.
- The client can see what changed and why it mattered.
- The result is measured in commercial or operational terms, not simply activity delivered.
- The client feels challenged, energised and more capable, not dependent or confused.

## The role of The Anchor

The Anchor is important evidence, but it is not Orange Jelly's category.

It is a live operating environment in which ideas have been tested against real customers, staff, suppliers, inventory, premises, regulation, margins, bookings and cash flow. That makes it a valuable proof laboratory for small-business growth.

The story should be framed as:

> Orange Jelly's thinking has been developed and tested inside a real trading business, where ideas have to work in practice and results have real consequences.

The story should not be framed as:

> Orange Jelly knows pubs, therefore it only solves pub problems.

The transferable value is the way problems are diagnosed, connected and solved.

## Long-term ambition

Orange Jelly should become known as the company an ambitious owner or managing director calls when:

- Growth has stalled.
- The business has become more complex than its current systems.
- The team is busy but the numbers are not moving enough.
- A serious opportunity exists but the route to capture it is unclear.
- The leader knows something has to change and wants an outside partner capable of challenging and building.

The ambition is not to become the biggest consultancy. It is to become one of the most useful, inventive and impactful growth partners for smaller businesses.

## Internal rallying cry

**Hear what is really happening. Find what matters. Make change happen.**

# 02. Positioning and Messaging

**Growth partner for ambitious small and mid-sized businesses.**

This is the clearest external category because it communicates both the outcome and the working relationship.

For search, procurement or explanatory contexts, Orange Jelly may also use:

**Technology-enabled business growth consultancy.**

The two phrases perform different jobs:

- `Growth partner` is human, active and commercially attractive.
- `Business growth consultancy` helps people understand and find the category.

Neither should be replaced with `AI consultancy` or `marketing agency` as the master description.

For ambitious small and mid-sized businesses that have built something real but reached a ceiling, Orange Jelly is a hands-on growth partner that exposes what is blocking progress and builds practical solutions across demand, conversion, margin, operations and customer experience.

Unlike agencies that sell a fixed service, Orange Jelly starts with the business problem and uses the right combination of creativity, technology, AI and commercial thinking to make measurable change happen.

Orange Jelly should describe capabilities through the problems they solve rather than a long agency service list.

Reach the right people, sharpen the proposition and build a stronger route into the business.

Possible work includes brand, marketing strategy, campaigns, content, acquisition, local visibility and partnerships.

Improve the journey from interest to enquiry, booking, purchase, repeat purchase or higher value.

Possible work includes websites, booking journeys, sales processes, CRM, customer communications, pricing presentation and funnel optimisation.

Find where value is leaking and improve the commercial model.

### 5. Improve the experience

Create a better experience for customers and the people delivering it.

Possible work includes service design, customer research, communications, digital touchpoints, training, feedback loops and experience standards.

### 6. Build for scale

Create the systems, information and capabilities needed for the next stage of growth.

Possible work includes dashboards, data foundations, repeatable playbooks, technology architecture, AI-enabled workflows and scalable operating models.

# 03. Ideal Client Profile and Fit

## The ideal client

Orange Jelly is for an ambitious small or mid-sized business that has already built something real and now needs a serious partner to help it reach the next stage.

The ideal client is not defined only by headcount or turnover. Fit is determined by the combination of:

- A meaningful growth problem
- Enough complexity for external thinking to add value
- Budget to implement change
- Access to a genuine decision maker
- Willingness to be challenged
- Capacity to act once a direction is agreed

As a commercial guide, the likely market is businesses with roughly 10 to 500 employees. This is Orange Jelly's practical market definition, not a formal legal definition of an SME. Smaller businesses may still fit where there is genuine scale, budget, urgency and ambition.

## Who should recognise themselves

The owner, founder, managing director or senior leader should be able to say:

- We have built a decent business, but growth has slowed or become harder.
- We are busy, but the numbers are not moving in proportion to the effort.
- We know things need to change, but the problem crosses several functions.
- We have ideas, data and activity, but not enough clarity or momentum.
- Our current systems were built for a smaller business.
- We need someone who can challenge us and help build the answer.
- We are willing to try something different if the thinking is sound.
- We care about measurable impact, not simply receiving deliverables.

## The emotional state

The best client is under constructive pressure, not already beyond rescue.

They may feel:

- Frustrated by a growth ceiling
- Impatient with slow internal progress
- Concerned that competitors are moving faster
- Excited by an opportunity they cannot yet capture
- Overwhelmed by connected problems
- Sceptical of agencies that deliver activity without impact
- Ready to make a meaningful change

Orange Jelly should not build its proposition around distressed businesses with no budget or implementation capacity. Pain creates urgency, but complete financial distress often destroys the ability to act.

The sweet spot is:

> **A good business under pressure to become a better one.**

## Priority decision makers

The strongest relationships will normally involve direct access to one or more of:

- Owner or founder
- Managing director
- Chief executive
- Commercial director
- Operations director
- Marketing director
- Transformation or innovation lead with real authority

Orange Jelly does not need unrestricted decision-making power. It does need permission to diagnose honestly, propose boldly and speak directly with the people who can approve or enable change.

## Common buying triggers

- Revenue has plateaued.
- A new location, market, service or product needs to perform.
- Customer acquisition is expensive or inconsistent.
- Enquiries, leads, bookings or website traffic are not converting.
- Profit is weak despite healthy sales.
- Labour, suppliers, utilities or waste are eroding margin.
- Teams are relying on manual work and disconnected tools.
- The business has grown faster than its operating systems.
- The leadership team cannot agree on the real problem.
- A new owner, managing director or investor wants change.
- The business wants to use AI but cannot identify worthwhile applications.
- The company has data but not useful decisions.
- Competitors appear faster, sharper or easier to buy from.

## Positive fit signals

- The client describes a problem, not only a requested task.
- They can explain why the issue matters commercially.
- They are open about what is not working.
- They want challenge rather than reassurance.
- They can provide access to data, staff and customers where appropriate.
- A senior sponsor attends the important conversations.
- They are prepared to stop low-value activity.
- They understand that growth may require investment.
- They want implementation, not only recommendations.
- They are willing to measure before and after.

## Poor fit signals

- `We just need someone to post three times a week.`
- `We know exactly what we want, we only need a pair of hands.`
- `Nothing can really change because this is how we have always done it.`
- `Can you send a few free ideas and we will decide whether to start?`
- `We cannot give you access to the decision maker, data or team.`
- `We want guaranteed growth without changing anything internally.`
- `We do not know what success looks like and do not want to define it.`
- `We need the cheapest possible supplier.`
- `Please implement this technology because everybody is talking about it.`
- `We want validation for a decision that has already been made.`

## Not designed around

- Sole traders seeking low-cost execution
- Hobby businesses
- Organisations with no budget for implementation
- Businesses that want business as usual presented as transformation
- Large enterprises requiring long procurement cycles and narrow delivery roles, unless the engagement is deliberately structured around a specific autonomous team or business unit

The public website does not need to reject these audiences aggressively. The proposition, pricing, language and qualification process should naturally filter them.

## The access agreement

A suitable client should agree to:

1. Name the senior sponsor.
2. Share relevant performance data and operating information.
3. Allow Orange Jelly to hear from the people closest to the problem.
4. Agree the problem statement and success measures.
5. Review bold recommendations on their merits rather than defaulting to precedent.
6. Assign an internal owner for implementation where required.
7. Make decisions at an agreed pace.

## Fit statement for the website

> Orange Jelly is built for businesses big enough to have real growth problems and small enough to move fast. You will get the most from us if you have built something worth growing, can give us access to the people and information that matter, and are ready to make changes rather than commission more activity.

## Qualification questions

Every initial enquiry should answer:

1. What is happening in the business that has prompted the conversation now?
2. What do you believe is blocking growth?
3. What evidence supports that view?
4. What would a successful outcome look like?
5. What happens if nothing changes?
6. Who owns the decision?
7. Who will help implement the change?
8. What has already been tried?
9. What constraints are genuinely fixed?
10. What level of investment is available if the opportunity is compelling?

## The ideal client in one sentence

**An ambitious leader who has built a real business, feels the ceiling, and is ready to be challenged by a partner capable of building the next move.**

# 04. Brand Identity and Platform

## Brand essence

**Brilliant change, made real.**

Orange Jelly should feel inventive and surprising, but never vague. The brand earns attention through personality and earns trust through commercial clarity, evidence and execution.

## Brand promise

**You bring the growth problem. We build the solution.**

## Brand idea

**An unfair advantage for ambitious businesses.**

This captures the value of combining creative thinking, commercial judgement, technology and AI in a way that a smaller business would struggle to assemble alone.

## Internal rallying cry

**Make the right change happen.**

A more provocative campaign or founder-led expression may be:

**Stop circling the problem. Make the f\*\*\*ing change.**

This should be used selectively, not as the permanent corporate strapline.

## Brand personality

### Brilliant

Sharp thinking, high standards and unexpectedly good solutions. Orange Jelly should create the reaction: `I had not thought about it like that.`

Brilliant does not mean showing off. It means seeing connections, simplifying complexity and producing work that changes the outcome.

### Bold

Willing to challenge assumptions, recommend meaningful change and say when an activity is not working.

Bold does not mean reckless. Recommendations should be grounded in evidence, context and commercial reality.

### Curious

Interested in how the whole business works, not only the function named in the brief. Orange Jelly asks better questions, hears what people actually mean and follows evidence across departmental boundaries.

### Practical

Ideas must survive contact with the real business. Orange Jelly values implementation, adoption, measurement and useful systems over theatre.

### Human

Warm, generous, straightforward and enjoyable to work with. The brand can be challenging without becoming cold, arrogant or combative.

### Restless

Uncomfortable with waste, inertia and `the way we have always done it`. Orange Jelly looks for a better route and is prepared to test it.

## Brand behaviours

1. **Hear deeply.** Do not confuse collecting information with understanding the business.
2. **Name the real problem.** Address root causes rather than accepting the first requested task.
3. **Challenge early.** Say the difficult thing before time and money are wasted.
4. **Use the right tool.** Choose AI, technology, marketing or a basic process change according to the problem.
5. **Build what matters.** Favour useful, implemented solutions over impressive-looking output.
6. **Prove the difference.** Agree measures, show impact and be honest when evidence is incomplete.
7. **Leave capability behind.** Improve the client's ability to operate, not only its dependence on Orange Jelly.

## The central brand tension

Orange Jelly should combine:

- Serious commercial outcomes with an unexpected name
- Intelligent thinking with plain language
- Bold challenge with warmth
- New technology with practical judgement
- Strategic scope with hands-on delivery
- Creativity with measurement

That tension is the identity. Removing either side would make the brand less distinctive.

## Brand archetype

A useful design shorthand is:

- **Primary: The Magician** - turns overlooked possibilities into tangible change.
- **Secondary: The Challenger** - confronts inertia and weak assumptions.
- **Supporting: The Ally** - works closely with leaders and teams rather than speaking down to them.

This is guidance, not language that should appear publicly.

## Visual identity direction

The visual system should feel **serious about outcomes but not self-important**.

### Core visual principles

- High contrast and strong hierarchy
- Bold, declarative typography
- A clean grid with moments of controlled disruption
- Orange used as a proprietary signal for action, pressure or focus
- Generous space around important statements
- Evidence and numbers treated as design assets
- Motion, stretch, pressure and connection used as graphic themes

### Suggested palette direction

Retain orange as the unmistakable signature. Build the system around:

- A vivid, confident orange
- Near-black for authority and contrast
- Warm off-white for approachability
- One optional high-energy accent for campaigns or digital interactions

The exact palette should be validated for accessibility and against the existing logo before final approval.

### Typography direction

- A bold modern grotesk or display sans serif for headlines
- A highly legible sans serif for body copy and interfaces
- Strong weight contrast rather than decorative type
- Sentence case as the default
- Very limited use of all capitals

The typography should feel modern and intelligent without resembling a software start-up template.

### Graphic language

Potential devices include:

- Pressure-point maps
- Elastic or fluid forms that suggest movement and adaptation
- Lines connecting causes to outcomes
- Blocks that shift, stretch or break out of a grid
- Highlight marks that show focus or intervention
- Before-and-after data visualisations

Avoid turning `jelly` into a childish illustration system. The name can carry playfulness without the design becoming juvenile.

### Photography and imagery

Prioritise:

- Real leaders, teams, customers and working environments
- Details that show systems, decisions and action
- Candid, confident human moments
- Real prototypes, dashboards, workflows and outputs
- The Anchor as a live business case study, not the default visual world

Avoid:

- Robots, glowing brains and generic AI circuitry
- Stock handshakes and staged boardrooms
- Hospitality imagery across every page
- Abstract technology imagery with no connection to a business outcome
- Over-polished lifestyle photography that makes Orange Jelly look like a branding studio

## Logo and naming direction

The name `Orange Jelly` is an asset because it is memorable, warm and unexpected in a serious commercial category.

The brand should not apologise for it or over-explain it. The design system should create enough authority that the unusual name becomes a strength.

The descriptor should do the category work:

> **Orange Jelly**  
> Growth partner for ambitious small and mid-sized businesses

## Proof as part of identity

Orange Jelly should never separate brand personality from evidence. Bold claims must be followed quickly by one of:

- A measurable result
- A practical example
- A clear method
- A client quote
- A working prototype
- An honest explanation of how success will be measured

The brand should feel exciting because it is capable, not because it is loud.

## Brand test

Every major expression should pass these questions:

- Does this feel clear enough for a busy managing director?
- Does it show that Orange Jelly solves business problems rather than sells tasks?
- Is there a sense of inventive thinking?
- Does it feel commercially grounded?
- Could this be mistaken for a generic agency or AI consultancy?
- Is the next action obvious?
- Is the energy earned by substance?

# 05. Tone of Voice

## Voice in one sentence

**Bold, bright and commercially direct, with enough warmth to bring people with us and enough edge to make change happen.**

## The five voice principles

### 1. Direct

Say the important thing first. Use plain words, active verbs and short sentences. Do not make the reader decode what Orange Jelly does.

Instead of:

> We provide a range of innovative solutions designed to support organisations on their growth journey.

Write:

> Tell us what is blocking growth. We will help you find it and build the fix.

### 2. Intelligent

Show clear thinking without hiding behind jargon. Connect causes and consequences. Explain why a recommendation matters commercially.

Intelligence is demonstrated through insight, precision and useful structure, not long words.

### 3. Provocative

Challenge stale assumptions and low-value activity. Create productive discomfort where it helps the reader act.

The purpose is not to shock. It is to interrupt passive thinking.

Instead of:

> Consistent social media can support your business objectives.

Write:

> Posting more will not fix a broken proposition, a weak conversion journey or an offer nobody wants.

### 4. Practical

Move quickly from diagnosis to action. Show what could change, how it would work and how impact would be measured.

Avoid advice that sounds impressive but leaves the reader unsure what to do next.

### 5. Human

Write like an experienced person speaking to another experienced person. Use warmth, humour and contractions. Respect the pressure business leaders are under.

Challenge the problem, the assumption or the decision. Do not belittle the person.

## Tone by context

### Homepage

**Tone:** Bold, concise and confident.  
Lead with the problem and promise. Keep the explanation concrete.

### Problem pages

**Tone:** Diagnostic, empathetic and commercially sharp.  
Show that Orange Jelly understands both the visible symptoms and the connected causes.

### Insights

**Tone:** Generous, useful and challenging.  
Give real value, then show where expert diagnosis or implementation would materially improve the outcome.

### Case studies

**Tone:** Clear, factual and evidence-led.  
Use the structure: problem, intervention, result and transferability. Do not inflate claims.

### Proposals

**Tone:** Decisive, structured and calm.  
Define the problem, intended outcome, scope, responsibilities and measures.

### Workshops

**Tone:** Curious, candid and energetic.  
Ask difficult questions without performing cleverness.

### Social content

**Tone:** Founder-led, opinionated and conversational.  
This is the best place for a stronger edge and occasional expletives.

### Formal procurement or legal content

**Tone:** Precise, restrained and professional.  
Do not use swearing, hype or unnecessary personality.

## The expletive rule

Expletives are allowed as **high voltage, not wallpaper**.

Use them when they add conviction to a manifesto line, founder point of view or campaign statement. Do not use them to make weak copy feel bold.

Recommended form:

- `f***ing`

Guidelines:

- Usually no more than once in a short piece.
- Do not use in legal documents, contracts, accessibility text, formal procurement, job descriptions or standard case studies.
- Avoid in search titles and meta descriptions.
- Never direct an expletive at a client, employee, competitor or audience group.
- Do not use it before the message has earned the intensity.
- Keep a clean alternative available for conservative channels.

Good use:

> Stop circling the problem. Make the f\*\*\*ing change.

Poor use:

> We build f\*\*\*ing awesome AI solutions for f\*\*\*ing brilliant businesses.

## Sentence style

- Use British English.
- Never use an em dash.
- Prefer active voice.
- Keep most paragraphs short.
- Use contractions where they sound natural.
- Use sentence case for headings.
- Use one strong idea per sentence.
- Vary rhythm. A short sentence can carry force.
- Use `we` and `you` confidently.
- Use numbers and examples whenever they improve credibility.
- Avoid multiple exclamation marks.
- Do not use rhetorical questions in every section.

## Message structure

Most Orange Jelly copy should follow this sequence:

1. **Name the problem.**
2. **Show the commercial stakes.**
3. **Challenge the obvious or incomplete answer.**
4. **Explain how Orange Jelly approaches it.**
5. **Provide proof or a practical example.**
6. **Give one clear next action.**

### Orange Jelly language

# 06. The Orange Jelly Method

## Working name

**HEAR. EXPOSE. BUILD. PROVE.**

This is the recommended version-one method. It uses active, strong language and reflects the way Orange Jelly wants to work. The name can be refined later, but the operating logic should remain.

## Why a method matters

Orange Jelly's range could otherwise look unfocused. A named method shows that different solutions are connected by a consistent way of working.

The method answers:

- How can Orange Jelly work across marketing, margin, operations and technology without becoming a generalist supplier?
- What does a client actually experience?
- How does Orange Jelly avoid producing activity without impact?
- How does the business know when to stop?

The answer is a repeatable discipline that starts with the problem and ends with evidence.

## 1. HEAR

**Understand what is really happening, not only what the brief says.**

Orange Jelly gets close to the operating reality. It hears from the leader, the people doing the work, the data and, where useful, the customer.

Typical activity:

- Owner or leadership interviews
- Staff and stakeholder conversations
- Customer feedback or observation
- Review of commercial and performance data
- Journey, process and systems walkthroughs
- Review of existing plans, tools, suppliers and activity
- Identification of fixed constraints and assumed constraints

Outputs may include:

- Initial problem statement
- Current-state map
- Evidence gaps
- Baseline measures
- Stakeholder and customer themes

The discipline:

> Listening is not enough. Orange Jelly must hear the difference between what people say is happening and what the business evidence shows.

## 2. EXPOSE

**Find the pressure points, root causes and opportunities that matter.**

Orange Jelly connects the visible symptoms to the wider growth system. It challenges comfortable explanations and makes trade-offs clear.

Typical activity:

- Root-cause analysis
- Growth-system mapping
- Funnel and conversion analysis
- Margin and cost analysis
- Process and workflow diagnosis
- Customer journey review
- Technology and data assessment
- Opportunity sizing
- Prioritisation by impact, confidence, effort and speed

Outputs may include:

- Growth Pressure Map
- Agreed problem definition
- Prioritised opportunities
- Constraints and dependencies
- Success measures
- Recommended intervention

The discipline:

> Do not solve the loudest symptom. Expose the few pressure points that can materially change the outcome.

## 3. BUILD

**Design and implement the right solution.**

The solution may be creative, commercial, operational or technical. It may combine several disciplines. AI is used when it improves the answer, not because it is part of the sales pitch.

Possible builds include:

- A new proposition or campaign
- A website or conversion journey
- A lead, booking or sales system
- A pricing or product-mix change
- A reporting dashboard
- An internal workflow or automation
- A stock, inventory or procurement process
- An AI-enabled tool or assistant
- A customer communication programme
- A team operating rhythm, SOP or decision framework
- A supplier or cost optimisation plan

Build principles:

- Start with the smallest intervention capable of creating material change.
- Prototype early when uncertainty is high.
- Design for the people who must use the solution.
- Make ownership and dependencies explicit.
- Build measurement into the solution.
- Avoid unnecessary technology.
- Document what the client needs to operate after handover.

## 4. PROVE

**Measure the difference, improve the solution and embed what works.**

Orange Jelly agrees success before building, then tracks whether the intervention changed the relevant commercial or operational outcome.

Typical activity:

- Compare against the baseline
- Review leading and lagging indicators
- Test and iterate
- Capture qualitative feedback
- Resolve adoption issues
- Document lessons and operating guidance
- Decide whether to scale, adjust or stop

Outputs may include:

- Impact report
- Before-and-after measures
- Learning log
- Handover and operating guidance
- Scale recommendation
- Case-study evidence, subject to client permission

The discipline:

> Work is not complete because a deliverable was supplied. It is complete when the agreed change is implemented, understood and measured to the extent the engagement allows.

## The required starting agreement

Before material work begins, Orange Jelly and the client should agree:

1. The problem being solved
2. Why it matters now
3. The baseline or current evidence
4. What success looks like
5. What is within scope
6. Who can make decisions
7. Who owns implementation
8. What data and access are required
9. How progress will be reviewed
10. What would cause the work to stop, change or expand

## The Growth Pressure Map

This should become a recognisable Orange Jelly diagnostic asset. It maps the business across six connected areas:

1. Demand
2. Conversion
3. Margin
4. Operations
5. Experience
6. Scale

For each area, assess:

- Current performance
- Evidence quality
- Primary pressure points
- Commercial consequence
- Opportunity size
- Dependencies
- Recommended next move

The map is not a generic scorecard. It should be tailored to the client's business model and used to focus action.

## The method in one line

> **Hear the business. Expose the pressure points. Build the fix. Prove the impact.**

## What the method prevents

- Starting with a requested tactic before understanding the problem
- Treating every issue as marketing
- Using AI where it adds no value
- Delivering a strategy the client cannot implement
- Measuring activity instead of impact
- Continuing work after the useful outcome has been achieved
- Allowing the client to avoid necessary decisions

## Working artefacts

The method should eventually have templates for:

- Discovery interview guide
- Data request
- Current-state map
- Growth Pressure Map
- Problem statement
- Success scorecard
- Opportunity prioritisation
- Build brief
- Experiment plan
- Implementation tracker
- Impact report
- Handover pack

These operational templates are a later build. The method in this document is the strategic source of truth.

# 07. Offer Architecture

## Commercial principle

Orange Jelly does not sell a menu of disconnected services. It sells progress against a defined growth problem.

The offer architecture should make it easy for a client to enter, prove value and expand the relationship without reducing Orange Jelly to hourly tasks.

## Recommended offer ladder

### 1. Growth Diagnostic

**Purpose:** Find what is really blocking growth and agree the highest-value next move.

**Typical duration:** 2 to 3 weeks

**Best for:** A leadership team that knows growth is being constrained but does not have a clear, evidence-backed problem definition.

**Core activity:**

- Leadership and stakeholder interviews
- Review of available performance data
- Customer, journey, process and systems review as relevant
- Growth Pressure Map
- Root-cause and opportunity analysis
- Prioritisation
- Success measures

**Core outputs:**

- Agreed problem statement
- Growth Pressure Map
- Prioritised opportunities
- Recommended intervention
- 90-day roadmap
- Indicative implementation options and investment

**Commercial role:** This should normally be a paid fixed-fee engagement. Orange Jelly should not give away the complete diagnosis during sales.

### 2. Growth Sprint

**Purpose:** Solve one defined, high-priority growth problem and put the solution into use.

**Typical duration:** 4 to 8 weeks

**Best for:** A problem with a clear scope, sponsor and measurable outcome.

**Example sprints:**

- Improve website or booking conversion
- Build a lead or customer follow-up system
- Redesign pricing and product presentation
- Reduce manual reporting or operational workload
- Prototype an AI-enabled workflow
- Improve stock, supplier or margin control
- Launch a new proposition or demand campaign
- Repair a broken customer journey

**Core outputs:**

- Build brief
- Implemented solution or working prototype
- Adoption plan
- Measurement plan
- Initial impact review
- Handover documentation

**Commercial role:** Fixed scope and fixed fee where possible. Specialist development, media, software or external production costs should be separate and transparent.

### 3. Growth Partnership

**Purpose:** Act as an embedded growth partner across a rolling portfolio of priorities.

**Typical duration:** 3 to 12 months

**Best for:** An ambitious business with several connected pressure points and a leadership team willing to make sustained changes.

**Typical structure:**

- Direct owner or managing director access
- Monthly priority setting
- A live growth backlog
- A defined number of active initiatives
- Strategic challenge and hands-on delivery
- Access to a partner network where specialist work is required
- Monthly impact and decision review
- Quarterly reset against business goals

**Possible areas:**

Demand, conversion, margin, customer experience, operational systems, technology, AI, data and scale.

**Commercial role:** Monthly retainer with clear capacity, governance, exclusions and outcome measures. It should not become an unlimited task desk.

### 4. Transformation Programme

**Purpose:** Address a larger cross-functional growth challenge that requires several connected interventions.

**Typical duration:** 3 to 9 months

**Best for:** A business at a major inflection point, such as expansion, new ownership, a new operating model, a serious profitability challenge or a technology-enabled change.

**Typical elements:**

- Deep diagnostic
- Leadership alignment
- Programme roadmap
- Multiple build workstreams
- Team workshops and operating changes
- Data and measurement
- Specialist delivery partners
- Adoption and handover

**Commercial role:** Bespoke programme fee with staged decisions, clear governance and agreed client responsibilities.

## Recommended entry logic

- If the problem is unclear or disputed, start with the **Growth Diagnostic**.
- If the problem is clear and evidence-backed, move directly to a **Growth Sprint**.
- If the business has several connected priorities and wants ongoing challenge, use a **Growth Partnership**.
- If the change crosses functions, systems and teams at significant scale, define a **Transformation Programme**.

## What Orange Jelly may deliver

Orange Jelly can legitimately work across:

- Growth and commercial strategy
- Brand and proposition
- Marketing and demand
- Conversion and customer journeys
- Websites and booking systems
- CRM and communications
- Pricing and margin
- Procurement and supplier optimisation
- Operations and workflows
- Data and dashboards
- Automation and internal tools
- AI use cases and prototypes
- Team standards, playbooks and SOPs

These are capabilities, not standalone category labels.

## Work Orange Jelly should normally decline

- Ongoing social posting with no wider growth problem
- Low-value design or production tasks sold by the hour
- `Can you make us a chatbot?` with no defined business case
- Free strategic proposals disguised as a sales process
- Projects without access to a decision maker
- Projects where success cannot be defined or observed
- Work that requires the business to change nothing
- Unlimited retainer arrangements with no priority control
- Implementation where the client has no owner or capacity

## Commercial principles

1. **Sell outcomes and decisions, not time.** Time still matters for capacity, but it should not be the main value story.
2. **Charge for diagnosis.** Understanding the real problem is valuable work.
3. **Separate external costs.** Media, software, development partners and production should be explicit.
4. **Define client responsibilities.** Access, decisions, data and implementation ownership affect results.
5. **Set a minimum viable engagement.** Orange Jelly needs a commercial threshold that protects quality and focus.
6. **Limit active priorities.** A growth partnership should have a managed backlog, not simultaneous work on everything.
7. **Build proof into scope.** Baseline, measurement and learning should not be optional extras.
8. **Stop when the agreed outcome is reached.** Do not preserve revenue by manufacturing work.
9. **Expand through evidence.** The next engagement should follow from proven opportunity, not pressure selling.

## Suggested offer naming alternatives

The working names are deliberately clear. More branded options can be considered later:

- **Growth Diagnostic:** Pressure Point Diagnostic
- **Growth Sprint:** Build Sprint
- **Growth Partnership:** Embedded Growth Partner
- **Transformation Programme:** Growth Reset

Clarity should win over cleverness at launch.

## Proposal structure

Every proposal should include:

1. What we heard
2. The problem we believe needs solving
3. Why it matters
4. What success looks like
5. What Orange Jelly will do
6. What the client must provide
7. What is outside scope
8. How impact will be measured
9. Investment and payment structure
10. Decision points and next step

## Pricing decisions still required

This document intentionally does not set prices. Before launch, Orange Jelly must decide:

- Minimum diagnostic fee
- Minimum sprint value
- Minimum monthly partnership value
- Typical programme range
- Travel and on-site terms
- Partner and software mark-up policy
- Whether any performance-related component is appropriate

The pricing should filter low-commitment enquiries without making the business inaccessible to strong smaller clients.

# 08. Proof and Case Study Framework

Orange Jelly's new positioning is broader than hospitality, but much of its strongest existing evidence comes from The Anchor.

The Anchor should be presented as a **live small-business growth laboratory**, not as evidence that Orange Jelly is only a pub consultancy.

## The Anchor narrative

Recommended framing:

> The Anchor is a real trading business with staff, customers, suppliers, inventory, premises, regulation, bookings, marketing, margin pressure and cash-flow consequences. This framing highlights transferability:

- Demand generation
- Conversion
- Customer experience
- Pricing and product mix
- Labour and rota optimisation
- Supplier and utility spend
- Inventory and waste
- Booking and ordering systems
- Website and digital journeys
- Automation and AI
- Reporting and decision-making

Orange Jelly should build proof across five levels.

### 1. Measured business outcomes

The strongest evidence:

- Revenue growth
- Profit or margin improvement
- Conversion improvement
- Cost reduction that protects growth or investment capacity
- Labour productivity
- Booking or lead growth
- Reduced no-shows or lost opportunities
- Faster cycle time
- Increased repeat purchase
- Improved capacity or utilisation

### 2. Operational outcomes

- Hours of manual work removed
- Process steps reduced
- Error rates reduced
- Better stock accuracy
- Faster reporting
- Improved decision speed
- Greater adoption of a system
- Clearer accountability

### 3. Customer outcomes

- Easier booking or purchase journey
- Higher satisfaction
- Fewer complaints
- Faster response
- Better retention
- Stronger reviews
- Improved customer understanding

### 4. Artefact proof

- Before-and-after journeys
- Screenshots
- Dashboards
- Prototypes
- Process maps
- Campaign creative
- Operating playbooks
- AI tools

### 5. Human testimony

- Client quotes
- Team feedback
- Customer feedback
- Referral statements
- Evidence that Orange Jelly challenged thinking and made implementation possible

## Existing proof candidates

The current website has previously referenced figures including:

- 403% increase in table bookings
- 567% increase in private hire bookings
- 98% increase in food revenue
- 828% increase in Google search visibility
- 89% reduction in no-shows

These should be treated as **proof candidates, not automatically approved claims**. Before publication, verify:

- Exact baseline and comparison period
- Whether the measure is absolute or percentage change
- Data source
- Other changes that may have influenced the result
- Date range
- Whether the result is still current
- Whether the claim can be explained clearly and fairly

## Case study structure

Every full case study should use the same structure.

### 1. The business context

What kind of business was this? What stage, constraints and commercial reality mattered?

### 2. The growth problem

What was happening? What was the visible symptom? What were the stakes?

### 3. What we heard

What did leaders, staff, customers and data reveal?

### 4. What we exposed

What was actually blocking growth? Which assumptions or connected issues mattered?

### 5. What we built

Describe the intervention in plain language. Include marketing, technology, process and commercial elements where relevant.

### 6. What changed

Use verified measures, dates and context. Separate leading indicators from final outcomes.

### 7. What made it transferable

Explain the general business principle so the case study does not feel sector-limited.

### 8. Client perspective

Include a quote about challenge, quality, implementation and impact.

### 9. Next move

Link to the most relevant growth problem or diagnostic offer.

## Short case study format

For homepage cards:

**Problem**  
One sentence describing the growth blockage.

**Build**  
One sentence explaining what Orange Jelly changed.

**Impact**  
One verified result.

**Transferable lesson**  
One line showing why it matters beyond the sector.

## Testimonial prompts

Ask clients:

1. What problem led you to Orange Jelly?
2. What did Orange Jelly understand that others had missed?
3. Where did the team challenge your thinking?
4. What did Orange Jelly actually build or change?
5. What impact have you seen?
6. How did it feel to work with the team?
7. What would you say to another business considering Orange Jelly?

The ideal answer should support the desired reputation:

> They are brilliant people with brilliant ideas. They challenged us, took us into spaces we had not considered and made change happen. We could see the impact.

## Evidence collection standard

At the start of every engagement:

- Record baseline measures.
- Agree the result definitions.
- Identify data quality gaps.
- Capture the original process or journey.
- Obtain permission for anonymised or named case-study use.

During delivery:

- Save important decisions and artefacts.
- Record tests and learning.
- Capture adoption and qualitative feedback.
- Track confounding changes.

At completion:

- Produce an impact summary.
- Ask for a testimonial while the value is fresh.
- Agree what can be published.
- Create a short and long case-study version.

To support the wider positioning, Orange Jelly should deliberately build case studies beyond hospitality across at least three categories:

1. Demand or conversion
2. Operations, margin or systems
3. AI or technology-enabled scale

The first non-hospitality cases should be selected partly for their ability to demonstrate this breadth.

## Proof principle

**Do not claim to be impactful. Show the problem, the change and the evidence.**

# 11. Brand and Opportunity Decision Filter

## Why this exists

Orange Jelly's breadth is a strength only when every decision reinforces the same promise. This filter prevents the business from drifting back into low-value agency work, random technology projects or content that attracts attention without creating suitable demand.

## Opportunity fit score

Score each statement from 0 to 2:

- `0` = No
- `1` = Partly or unclear
- `2` = Yes

### Client and problem

1. There is a meaningful commercial or operational growth problem.
2. The problem matters enough to justify serious change and investment.
3. A senior decision maker will be directly involved.
4. The client is willing to be challenged and consider unfamiliar options.
5. The client can provide the information, access and implementation support required.

### Work and impact

6. Orange Jelly can make a material difference rather than simply complete a task.
7. Success can be defined and measured with reasonable honesty.
8. The engagement uses Orange Jelly's strength at the intersection of creativity, commerce and technology.
9. The work can produce useful learning, proof or reusable capability.
10. The commercial terms support high-quality work and proper focus.

## Score interpretation

- **17 to 20:** Strong fit. Progress confidently.
- **13 to 16:** Potential fit. Resolve conditions before proposing.
- **9 to 12:** Weak fit. Reshape the engagement or decline.
- **0 to 8:** Decline. The work is likely to distract, underperform or damage positioning.

A high score does not remove the need for judgement. A single zero on decision-maker access, willingness to change or commercial viability may still be disqualifying.

## Red-line questions

Decline or pause when:

- The client wants a tactic but refuses to discuss the problem.
- The sponsor cannot access the people or data required.
- The organisation wants Orange Jelly to validate a fixed answer.
- Implementation depends on internal changes nobody will own.
- The client expects guaranteed outcomes while controlling the inputs.
- The project is being bought only because AI is fashionable.
- The work would make Orange Jelly look like a cheap task supplier.
- The likely impact is too small to justify the engagement.

## Solution filter

Before recommending any solution, ask:

1. Which agreed problem does this solve?
2. Which measure should move if it works?
3. Is this a root-cause intervention or a symptom treatment?
4. Is there a simpler route?
5. Does technology genuinely improve the answer?
6. Does AI genuinely improve the answer?
7. Can the client's team use and sustain it?
8. What must stop, change or be funded for this to work?
9. How quickly can we test the riskiest assumption?
10. What evidence would make us stop?

## Content filter

Before creating an article, page, video or campaign, ask:

- Does it address a real growth problem or decision?
- Is the intended audience commercially attractive?
- Does it reinforce Orange Jelly as a growth partner?
- Could it accidentally position the business as hospitality-only, social-media-only or AI-only?
- Does it contain an original or useful point of view?
- Is there relevant proof?
- Is there a logical next action?
- Can performance be measured beyond views or visits?

## Brand filter

Before approving copy or design, ask:

- Is it clear?
- Is it bold for a reason?
- Is it commercially grounded?
- Is it warm enough to be inviting?
- Does it show that Orange Jelly can build as well as advise?
- Is AI presented as a tool rather than a gimmick?
- Does it feel distinctive from a generic agency?
- Would a managing director trust it?
- Would the right client feel challenged and excited?
- Is there evidence behind the strongest claim?

## The final test

> **Does this help Orange Jelly become known for brilliant, measurable change, or does it merely keep Orange Jelly busy?**

# 13. Open Decisions

This document separates confirmed strategy from choices that still need approval. It prevents designers, developers or AI tools from making permanent decisions by accident.

## Decisions required before website copy is final

### 1. Final category pairing

**Recommended default:**

- Headline category: `Growth partner for ambitious small and mid-sized businesses`
- Supporting/search category: `Technology-enabled business growth consultancy`

Decision: Confirm whether `consultancy` should appear visibly on the homepage or mainly in metadata and explanatory copy.

### 2. Final core line

**Recommended default:**

`You bring the growth problem. We build the solution.`

Decision: Confirm this as the permanent brand promise rather than a launch campaign line.

### 3. Signature method name

**Recommended default:**

`HEAR. EXPOSE. BUILD. PROVE.`

Decision: Test whether `EXPOSE` feels constructively challenging or too aggressive in client conversations.

### 4. Primary call to action

**Recommended default:**

- Button: `Book a growth diagnostic`
- Campaign or section line: `Bring us the problem`

Decision: Confirm whether the first step is always a paid diagnostic or whether selected enquiries can enter through a free fit call.

### 5. Target business size

**Recommended default:**

`Typically 10 to 500 employees, with fit based on complexity, budget, authority and ambition rather than headcount alone.`

Decision: Choose whether this range should be public, used only for qualification, or replaced with revenue bands.

### 6. Minimum commercial threshold

Decision required:

- Minimum diagnostic fee
- Minimum sprint value
- Minimum monthly partnership value
- Minimum programme value

This is one of the most important filters against low-value work.

### 7. Sector strategy

**Recommended default:**

Remain sector-open at the top level. Present hospitality as a proof-rich specialist area. Add other sector pages only after credible evidence exists.

Decision: Identify two or three sectors where Peter's network, credibility or problem set creates the best near-term route to non-hospitality revenue.

### 8. Geographic scope

Decision: UK only, UK-first with international work, or fully international.

The website, pricing, legal terms and search strategy depend on this.

### 9. Founder versus company brand

**Recommended default:**

Lead with Orange Jelly as the company and use Peter prominently as founder, practitioner and point of view.

Decision: Determine how much delivery is expected to remain founder-led and how quickly a visible team or partner network needs to be established.

### 10. Delivery model

Decision required:

- What Orange Jelly builds directly
- What is delivered through specialist partners
- Whether partners are white-labelled, introduced or subcontracted
- Quality and commercial standards
- Ownership of client relationships

### 11. Swearing boundary

**Recommended default:**

Use partially censored expletives sparingly in founder-led social, manifesto content and selected campaign lines. Keep core service, proposal and formal content clean.

Decision: Confirm whether any expletive appears on the permanent homepage.

### 12. Visual identity scope

Decision: Evolution of the existing identity or a complete redesign.

Before deciding, review:

- Current logo equity
- Legibility
- Digital flexibility
- Whether the current look reinforces hospitality or agency associations
- Ability to support a more commercially authoritative brand

### 13. Proof approval

Decision required for every claimed result:

- Verified number
- Baseline
- Date range
- Data source
- Context
- Permission

No existing percentage should be copied into the new site until this is complete.

### 14. Legacy hospitality content

**Recommended default:**

Keep high-value content, move it into a hospitality insight hub, update the Orange Jelly description and strengthen commercial pathways.

Decision: Which pages remain strategically valuable and which should be redirected or removed.

### 15. Search priorities

Decision required after keyword research:

- First three problem clusters
- Priority industries
- Geographic modifiers
- Search tools or calculators to build
- Balance between high-intent commercial pages and broader authority content

## Decisions that can wait until after launch

- Final names for every offer
- A larger proprietary research programme
- Additional sector hubs
- A formal certification or partner programme
- A complex membership or subscription product
- A broad productised AI catalogue
- A complete library of operational templates

## Decision rule

When uncertain, choose the option that most clearly reinforces:

1. Growth as the outcome
2. Problem-first diagnosis
3. Practical build capability
4. Measurable impact
5. Ambitious clients ready to change

Do not choose based only on what is easiest to sell this week if it recreates the positioning problem Orange Jelly is trying to escape.
