# Instructions for AI coding and content agents

Read `README.md` and documents 01 to 06 before generating any Orange Jelly website structure, copy, design direction or product requirements.

## Non-negotiable positioning rules

- Describe Orange Jelly as a **growth partner for ambitious small and mid-sized businesses**.
- Do not describe Orange Jelly as a hospitality marketing agency, social media agency, digital agency, AI support service or generic AI consultancy.
- Hospitality is proof and an initial area of experience. It is not the boundary of the offer.
- Start with the client's growth problem. Do not start with a list of services.
- Present marketing, technology, AI, automation, commercial analysis and operational improvement as tools selected to solve the problem.
- State that AI is part of the toolkit, not the product.
- Do not make unverified performance claims. Use placeholders where evidence has not been approved.

## Writing rules

- Use British English.
- Never use an em dash.
- Use active voice, short sentences and concrete language.
- Avoid corporate filler, jargon and generic claims about innovation.
- Explain what changes, how it helps and how impact will be measured.
- Expletives may be used sparingly in founder-led or manifesto content. Use a partially censored form such as `f***ing`. Do not use them as decoration.
- Never swear at, about or on behalf of a client, employee or customer.

## Website rules

- Build the information architecture around growth problems, method, proof and fit.
- The primary action should be problem-led, such as `Bring us the problem` or `Book a growth diagnostic`.
- Do not ask a visitor to choose a service before Orange Jelly understands the problem.
- Every insight article must have a clear commercial job, a relevant proof point and a useful next step.
- Retain useful hospitality content, but contain it within a sector or insight structure rather than allowing it to define the whole business.

## Page brief required before copy generation

For every page, specify:

1. Audience
2. Problem or decision the page addresses
3. Desired visitor action
4. Required proof
5. Relevant offer
6. Search intent
7. Claims that require verification

## Conflict handling

If a requested output conflicts with the brand system, flag the conflict rather than silently reverting to the old position. Check `13-open-decisions.md` before making assumptions.
