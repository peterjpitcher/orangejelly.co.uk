# 08. Proof and Case Study Framework

## The proof problem

Orange Jelly's new positioning is broader than hospitality, but much of its strongest existing evidence comes from The Anchor.

The answer is not to hide that proof. It is to frame it correctly.

The Anchor should be presented as a **live small-business growth laboratory**, not as evidence that Orange Jelly is only a pub consultancy.

## The Anchor narrative

Recommended framing:

> The Anchor is a real trading business with staff, customers, suppliers, inventory, premises, regulation, bookings, marketing, margin pressure and cash-flow consequences. It gives Orange Jelly somewhere to test ideas in the real world, where clever thinking only matters if it changes the numbers or improves how the business operates.

This framing highlights transferability:

- Demand generation
- Conversion
- Customer experience
- Pricing and product mix
- Labour and rota optimisation
- Supplier and utility spend
- Inventory and waste
- Booking and ordering systems
- Website and digital journeys
- Automation and AI
- Reporting and decision-making

## Proof hierarchy

Orange Jelly should build proof across five levels.

### 1. Measured business outcomes

The strongest evidence:

- Revenue growth
- Profit or margin improvement
- Conversion improvement
- Cost reduction that protects growth or investment capacity
- Labour productivity
- Booking or lead growth
- Reduced no-shows or lost opportunities
- Faster cycle time
- Increased repeat purchase
- Improved capacity or utilisation

### 2. Operational outcomes

- Hours of manual work removed
- Process steps reduced
- Error rates reduced
- Better stock accuracy
- Faster reporting
- Improved decision speed
- Greater adoption of a system
- Clearer accountability

### 3. Customer outcomes

- Easier booking or purchase journey
- Higher satisfaction
- Fewer complaints
- Faster response
- Better retention
- Stronger reviews
- Improved customer understanding

### 4. Artefact proof

- Before-and-after journeys
- Screenshots
- Dashboards
- Prototypes
- Process maps
- Campaign creative
- Operating playbooks
- AI tools

### 5. Human testimony

- Client quotes
- Team feedback
- Customer feedback
- Referral statements
- Evidence that Orange Jelly challenged thinking and made implementation possible

## Existing proof candidates

The current website has previously referenced figures including:

- 403% increase in table bookings
- 567% increase in private hire bookings
- 98% increase in food revenue
- 828% increase in Google search visibility
- 89% reduction in no-shows

These should be treated as **proof candidates, not automatically approved claims**. Before publication, verify:

- Exact baseline and comparison period
- Whether the measure is absolute or percentage change
- Data source
- Other changes that may have influenced the result
- Date range
- Whether the result is still current
- Whether the claim can be explained clearly and fairly

## Case study structure

Every full case study should use the same structure.

### 1. The business context

What kind of business was this? What stage, constraints and commercial reality mattered?

### 2. The growth problem

What was happening? What was the visible symptom? What were the stakes?

### 3. What we heard

What did leaders, staff, customers and data reveal?

### 4. What we exposed

What was actually blocking growth? Which assumptions or connected issues mattered?

### 5. What we built

Describe the intervention in plain language. Include marketing, technology, process and commercial elements where relevant.

### 6. What changed

Use verified measures, dates and context. Separate leading indicators from final outcomes.

### 7. What made it transferable

Explain the general business principle so the case study does not feel sector-limited.

### 8. Client perspective

Include a quote about challenge, quality, implementation and impact.

### 9. Next move

Link to the most relevant growth problem or diagnostic offer.

## Short case study format

For homepage cards:

**Problem**  
One sentence describing the growth blockage.

**Build**  
One sentence explaining what Orange Jelly changed.

**Impact**  
One verified result.

**Transferable lesson**  
One line showing why it matters beyond the sector.

## Testimonial prompts

Ask clients:

1. What problem led you to Orange Jelly?
2. What did Orange Jelly understand that others had missed?
3. Where did the team challenge your thinking?
4. What did Orange Jelly actually build or change?
5. What impact have you seen?
6. How did it feel to work with the team?
7. What would you say to another business considering Orange Jelly?

The ideal answer should support the desired reputation:

> They are brilliant people with brilliant ideas. They challenged us, took us into spaces we had not considered and made change happen. We could see the impact.

## Evidence collection standard

At the start of every engagement:

- Record baseline measures.
- Agree the result definitions.
- Identify data quality gaps.
- Capture the original process or journey.
- Obtain permission for anonymised or named case-study use.

During delivery:

- Save important decisions and artefacts.
- Record tests and learning.
- Capture adoption and qualitative feedback.
- Track confounding changes.

At completion:

- Produce an impact summary.
- Ask for a testimonial while the value is fresh.
- Agree what can be published.
- Create a short and long case-study version.

## Proof gaps to close

To support the wider positioning, Orange Jelly should deliberately build case studies beyond hospitality across at least three categories:

1. Demand or conversion
2. Operations, margin or systems
3. AI or technology-enabled scale

The first non-hospitality cases should be selected partly for their ability to demonstrate this breadth.

## Proof principle

**Do not claim to be impactful. Show the problem, the change and the evidence.**
