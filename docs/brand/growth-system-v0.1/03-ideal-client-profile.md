# 03. Ideal Client Profile and Fit

## The ideal client

Orange Jelly is for an ambitious small or mid-sized business that has already built something real and now needs a serious partner to help it reach the next stage.

The ideal client is not defined only by headcount or turnover. Fit is determined by the combination of:

- A meaningful growth problem
- Enough complexity for external thinking to add value
- Budget to implement change
- Access to a genuine decision maker
- Willingness to be challenged
- Capacity to act once a direction is agreed

As a commercial guide, the likely market is businesses with roughly 10 to 500 employees. This is Orange Jelly's practical market definition, not a formal legal definition of an SME. Smaller businesses may still fit where there is genuine scale, budget, urgency and ambition.

## Who should recognise themselves

The owner, founder, managing director or senior leader should be able to say:

- We have built a decent business, but growth has slowed or become harder.
- We are busy, but the numbers are not moving in proportion to the effort.
- We know things need to change, but the problem crosses several functions.
- We have ideas, data and activity, but not enough clarity or momentum.
- Our current systems were built for a smaller business.
- We need someone who can challenge us and help build the answer.
- We are willing to try something different if the thinking is sound.
- We care about measurable impact, not simply receiving deliverables.

## The emotional state

The best client is under constructive pressure, not already beyond rescue.

They may feel:

- Frustrated by a growth ceiling
- Impatient with slow internal progress
- Concerned that competitors are moving faster
- Excited by an opportunity they cannot yet capture
- Overwhelmed by connected problems
- Sceptical of agencies that deliver activity without impact
- Ready to make a meaningful change

Orange Jelly should not build its proposition around distressed businesses with no budget or implementation capacity. Pain creates urgency, but complete financial distress often destroys the ability to act.

The sweet spot is:

> **A good business under pressure to become a better one.**

## Priority decision makers

The strongest relationships will normally involve direct access to one or more of:

- Owner or founder
- Managing director
- Chief executive
- Commercial director
- Operations director
- Marketing director
- Transformation or innovation lead with real authority

Orange Jelly does not need unrestricted decision-making power. It does need permission to diagnose honestly, propose boldly and speak directly with the people who can approve or enable change.

## Common buying triggers

- Revenue has plateaued.
- A new location, market, service or product needs to perform.
- Customer acquisition is expensive or inconsistent.
- Enquiries, leads, bookings or website traffic are not converting.
- Profit is weak despite healthy sales.
- Labour, suppliers, utilities or waste are eroding margin.
- Teams are relying on manual work and disconnected tools.
- The business has grown faster than its operating systems.
- The leadership team cannot agree on the real problem.
- A new owner, managing director or investor wants change.
- The business wants to use AI but cannot identify worthwhile applications.
- The company has data but not useful decisions.
- Competitors appear faster, sharper or easier to buy from.

## Positive fit signals

- The client describes a problem, not only a requested task.
- They can explain why the issue matters commercially.
- They are open about what is not working.
- They want challenge rather than reassurance.
- They can provide access to data, staff and customers where appropriate.
- A senior sponsor attends the important conversations.
- They are prepared to stop low-value activity.
- They understand that growth may require investment.
- They want implementation, not only recommendations.
- They are willing to measure before and after.

## Poor fit signals

- `We just need someone to post three times a week.`
- `We know exactly what we want, we only need a pair of hands.`
- `Nothing can really change because this is how we have always done it.`
- `Can you send a few free ideas and we will decide whether to start?`
- `We cannot give you access to the decision maker, data or team.`
- `We want guaranteed growth without changing anything internally.`
- `We do not know what success looks like and do not want to define it.`
- `We need the cheapest possible supplier.`
- `Please implement this technology because everybody is talking about it.`
- `We want validation for a decision that has already been made.`

## Not designed around

- Sole traders seeking low-cost execution
- Hobby businesses
- Organisations with no budget for implementation
- Businesses that want business as usual presented as transformation
- Large enterprises requiring long procurement cycles and narrow delivery roles, unless the engagement is deliberately structured around a specific autonomous team or business unit

The public website does not need to reject these audiences aggressively. The proposition, pricing, language and qualification process should naturally filter them.

## The access agreement

A suitable client should agree to:

1. Name the senior sponsor.
2. Share relevant performance data and operating information.
3. Allow Orange Jelly to hear from the people closest to the problem.
4. Agree the problem statement and success measures.
5. Review bold recommendations on their merits rather than defaulting to precedent.
6. Assign an internal owner for implementation where required.
7. Make decisions at an agreed pace.

## Fit statement for the website

> Orange Jelly is built for businesses big enough to have real growth problems and small enough to move fast. You will get the most from us if you have built something worth growing, can give us access to the people and information that matter, and are ready to make changes rather than commission more activity.

## Qualification questions

Every initial enquiry should answer:

1. What is happening in the business that has prompted the conversation now?
2. What do you believe is blocking growth?
3. What evidence supports that view?
4. What would a successful outcome look like?
5. What happens if nothing changes?
6. Who owns the decision?
7. Who will help implement the change?
8. What has already been tried?
9. What constraints are genuinely fixed?
10. What level of investment is available if the opportunity is compelling?

## The ideal client in one sentence

**An ambitious leader who has built a real business, feels the ceiling, and is ready to be challenged by a partner capable of building the next move.**
