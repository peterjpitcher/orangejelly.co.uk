# 05. Tone of Voice

## Voice in one sentence

**Bold, bright and commercially direct, with enough warmth to bring people with us and enough edge to make change happen.**

## The five voice principles

### 1. Direct

Say the important thing first. Use plain words, active verbs and short sentences. Do not make the reader decode what Orange Jelly does.

Instead of:

> We provide a range of innovative solutions designed to support organisations on their growth journey.

Write:

> Tell us what is blocking growth. We will help you find it and build the fix.

### 2. Intelligent

Show clear thinking without hiding behind jargon. Connect causes and consequences. Explain why a recommendation matters commercially.

Intelligence is demonstrated through insight, precision and useful structure, not long words.

### 3. Provocative

Challenge stale assumptions and low-value activity. Create productive discomfort where it helps the reader act.

The purpose is not to shock. It is to interrupt passive thinking.

Instead of:

> Consistent social media can support your business objectives.

Write:

> Posting more will not fix a broken proposition, a weak conversion journey or an offer nobody wants.

### 4. Practical

Move quickly from diagnosis to action. Show what could change, how it would work and how impact would be measured.

Avoid advice that sounds impressive but leaves the reader unsure what to do next.

### 5. Human

Write like an experienced person speaking to another experienced person. Use warmth, humour and contractions. Respect the pressure business leaders are under.

Challenge the problem, the assumption or the decision. Do not belittle the person.

## Tone by context

### Homepage

**Tone:** Bold, concise and confident.  
Lead with the problem and promise. Keep the explanation concrete.

### Problem pages

**Tone:** Diagnostic, empathetic and commercially sharp.  
Show that Orange Jelly understands both the visible symptoms and the connected causes.

### Insights

**Tone:** Generous, useful and challenging.  
Give real value, then show where expert diagnosis or implementation would materially improve the outcome.

### Case studies

**Tone:** Clear, factual and evidence-led.  
Use the structure: problem, intervention, result and transferability. Do not inflate claims.

### Proposals

**Tone:** Decisive, structured and calm.  
Define the problem, intended outcome, scope, responsibilities and measures.

### Workshops

**Tone:** Curious, candid and energetic.  
Ask difficult questions without performing cleverness.

### Social content

**Tone:** Founder-led, opinionated and conversational.  
This is the best place for a stronger edge and occasional expletives.

### Formal procurement or legal content

**Tone:** Precise, restrained and professional.  
Do not use swearing, hype or unnecessary personality.

## The expletive rule

Expletives are allowed as **high voltage, not wallpaper**.

Use them when they add conviction to a manifesto line, founder point of view or campaign statement. Do not use them to make weak copy feel bold.

Recommended form:

- `f***ing`

Guidelines:

- Usually no more than once in a short piece.
- Do not use in legal documents, contracts, accessibility text, formal procurement, job descriptions or standard case studies.
- Avoid in search titles and meta descriptions.
- Never direct an expletive at a client, employee, competitor or audience group.
- Do not use it before the message has earned the intensity.
- Keep a clean alternative available for conservative channels.

Good use:

> Stop circling the problem. Make the f\*\*\*ing change.

Poor use:

> We build f\*\*\*ing awesome AI solutions for f\*\*\*ing brilliant businesses.

## Sentence style

- Use British English.
- Never use an em dash.
- Prefer active voice.
- Keep most paragraphs short.
- Use contractions where they sound natural.
- Use sentence case for headings.
- Use one strong idea per sentence.
- Vary rhythm. A short sentence can carry force.
- Use `we` and `you` confidently.
- Use numbers and examples whenever they improve credibility.
- Avoid multiple exclamation marks.
- Do not use rhetorical questions in every section.

## Message structure

Most Orange Jelly copy should follow this sequence:

1. **Name the problem.**
2. **Show the commercial stakes.**
3. **Challenge the obvious or incomplete answer.**
4. **Explain how Orange Jelly approaches it.**
5. **Provide proof or a practical example.**
6. **Give one clear next action.**

## Words and phrases to favour

- Growth problem
- Pressure point
- Make change happen
- Build the solution
- Expose what is blocking growth
- Practical AI
- Commercial impact
- Real trading business
- Measurable change
- Challenge
- Build
- Prove
- Demand
- Conversion
- Margin
- Operational drag
- Customer experience
- Scale
- Business big enough to matter and small enough to move

## Words and phrases to challenge or remove

- Innovative solutions
- Cutting-edge
- Game-changing
- Revolutionary
- Synergy
- Unlock your potential
- Growth journey
- 360-degree
- Full-service
- Best in class
- Thought leader
- Digital transformation, unless the specific change is explained
- AI-powered, unless AI materially changes the product or outcome
- Bespoke, when `built for the problem` is clearer

## Signature lines

These can be repeated across the brand:

- **You bring the growth problem. We build the solution.**
- **AI is part of the toolkit, not the product.**
- **A content calendar will not fix a broken growth system.**
- **Brilliant thinking is useless until it changes something.**
- **Big enough to have real growth problems. Small enough to move fast.**
- **We do not arrive with a pre-selected answer.**
- **We are not here to preserve business as usual.**
- **Hear the business. Expose the pressure points. Build the fix. Prove the impact.**

## Before and after examples

### Generic agency language

> We help SMEs leverage innovative digital technologies to unlock growth and improve efficiencies.

### Orange Jelly language

> Something is blocking growth. We get under the skin of the business, find the pressure points and build the right fix. Sometimes that means marketing. Sometimes it means systems, margin, operations or AI. The problem decides the tool.

### Generic AI language

> Our AI-powered solutions streamline processes and drive transformation.

### Orange Jelly language

> We use AI when it gives the business an advantage. When a simpler process will do the job better, we use that instead.

### Generic consultancy language

> Following a comprehensive discovery process, we deliver strategic recommendations aligned to your objectives.

### Orange Jelly language

> We hear what is really happening, agree what success looks like and identify the few changes most likely to move the numbers. Then we help build them.

## Voice quality check

Before publishing, ask:

- Is the problem clear in the first few lines?
- Could a busy owner understand this without industry jargon?
- Does the copy sound confident without pretending certainty we do not have?
- Have we explained the commercial consequence?
- Is there a real point of view?
- Does every bold line have substance behind it?
- Is the next action clear?
- Could this copy belong to any agency? If so, rewrite it.
