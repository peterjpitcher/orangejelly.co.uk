# 07. Offer Architecture

## Commercial principle

Orange Jelly does not sell a menu of disconnected services. It sells progress against a defined growth problem.

The offer architecture should make it easy for a client to enter, prove value and expand the relationship without reducing Orange Jelly to hourly tasks.

## Recommended offer ladder

### 1. Growth Diagnostic

**Purpose:** Find what is really blocking growth and agree the highest-value next move.

**Typical duration:** 2 to 3 weeks

**Best for:** A leadership team that knows growth is being constrained but does not have a clear, evidence-backed problem definition.

**Core activity:**

- Leadership and stakeholder interviews
- Review of available performance data
- Customer, journey, process and systems review as relevant
- Growth Pressure Map
- Root-cause and opportunity analysis
- Prioritisation
- Success measures

**Core outputs:**

- Agreed problem statement
- Growth Pressure Map
- Prioritised opportunities
- Recommended intervention
- 90-day roadmap
- Indicative implementation options and investment

**Commercial role:** This should normally be a paid fixed-fee engagement. Orange Jelly should not give away the complete diagnosis during sales.

### 2. Growth Sprint

**Purpose:** Solve one defined, high-priority growth problem and put the solution into use.

**Typical duration:** 4 to 8 weeks

**Best for:** A problem with a clear scope, sponsor and measurable outcome.

**Example sprints:**

- Improve website or booking conversion
- Build a lead or customer follow-up system
- Redesign pricing and product presentation
- Reduce manual reporting or operational workload
- Prototype an AI-enabled workflow
- Improve stock, supplier or margin control
- Launch a new proposition or demand campaign
- Repair a broken customer journey

**Core outputs:**

- Build brief
- Implemented solution or working prototype
- Adoption plan
- Measurement plan
- Initial impact review
- Handover documentation

**Commercial role:** Fixed scope and fixed fee where possible. Specialist development, media, software or external production costs should be separate and transparent.

### 3. Growth Partnership

**Purpose:** Act as an embedded growth partner across a rolling portfolio of priorities.

**Typical duration:** 3 to 12 months

**Best for:** An ambitious business with several connected pressure points and a leadership team willing to make sustained changes.

**Typical structure:**

- Direct owner or managing director access
- Monthly priority setting
- A live growth backlog
- A defined number of active initiatives
- Strategic challenge and hands-on delivery
- Access to a partner network where specialist work is required
- Monthly impact and decision review
- Quarterly reset against business goals

**Possible areas:**

Demand, conversion, margin, customer experience, operational systems, technology, AI, data and scale.

**Commercial role:** Monthly retainer with clear capacity, governance, exclusions and outcome measures. It should not become an unlimited task desk.

### 4. Transformation Programme

**Purpose:** Address a larger cross-functional growth challenge that requires several connected interventions.

**Typical duration:** 3 to 9 months

**Best for:** A business at a major inflection point, such as expansion, new ownership, a new operating model, a serious profitability challenge or a technology-enabled change.

**Typical elements:**

- Deep diagnostic
- Leadership alignment
- Programme roadmap
- Multiple build workstreams
- Team workshops and operating changes
- Data and measurement
- Specialist delivery partners
- Adoption and handover

**Commercial role:** Bespoke programme fee with staged decisions, clear governance and agreed client responsibilities.

## Recommended entry logic

- If the problem is unclear or disputed, start with the **Growth Diagnostic**.
- If the problem is clear and evidence-backed, move directly to a **Growth Sprint**.
- If the business has several connected priorities and wants ongoing challenge, use a **Growth Partnership**.
- If the change crosses functions, systems and teams at significant scale, define a **Transformation Programme**.

## What Orange Jelly may deliver

Orange Jelly can legitimately work across:

- Growth and commercial strategy
- Brand and proposition
- Marketing and demand
- Conversion and customer journeys
- Websites and booking systems
- CRM and communications
- Pricing and margin
- Procurement and supplier optimisation
- Operations and workflows
- Data and dashboards
- Automation and internal tools
- AI use cases and prototypes
- Team standards, playbooks and SOPs

These are capabilities, not standalone category labels.

## Work Orange Jelly should normally decline

- Ongoing social posting with no wider growth problem
- Low-value design or production tasks sold by the hour
- `Can you make us a chatbot?` with no defined business case
- Free strategic proposals disguised as a sales process
- Projects without access to a decision maker
- Projects where success cannot be defined or observed
- Work that requires the business to change nothing
- Unlimited retainer arrangements with no priority control
- Implementation where the client has no owner or capacity

## Commercial principles

1. **Sell outcomes and decisions, not time.** Time still matters for capacity, but it should not be the main value story.
2. **Charge for diagnosis.** Understanding the real problem is valuable work.
3. **Separate external costs.** Media, software, development partners and production should be explicit.
4. **Define client responsibilities.** Access, decisions, data and implementation ownership affect results.
5. **Set a minimum viable engagement.** Orange Jelly needs a commercial threshold that protects quality and focus.
6. **Limit active priorities.** A growth partnership should have a managed backlog, not simultaneous work on everything.
7. **Build proof into scope.** Baseline, measurement and learning should not be optional extras.
8. **Stop when the agreed outcome is reached.** Do not preserve revenue by manufacturing work.
9. **Expand through evidence.** The next engagement should follow from proven opportunity, not pressure selling.

## Suggested offer naming alternatives

The working names are deliberately clear. More branded options can be considered later:

- **Growth Diagnostic:** Pressure Point Diagnostic
- **Growth Sprint:** Build Sprint
- **Growth Partnership:** Embedded Growth Partner
- **Transformation Programme:** Growth Reset

Clarity should win over cleverness at launch.

## Proposal structure

Every proposal should include:

1. What we heard
2. The problem we believe needs solving
3. Why it matters
4. What success looks like
5. What Orange Jelly will do
6. What the client must provide
7. What is outside scope
8. How impact will be measured
9. Investment and payment structure
10. Decision points and next step

## Pricing decisions still required

This document intentionally does not set prices. Before launch, Orange Jelly must decide:

- Minimum diagnostic fee
- Minimum sprint value
- Minimum monthly partnership value
- Typical programme range
- Travel and on-site terms
- Partner and software mark-up policy
- Whether any performance-related component is appropriate

The pricing should filter low-commitment enquiries without making the business inaccessible to strong smaller clients.
