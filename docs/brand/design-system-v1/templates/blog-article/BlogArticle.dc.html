Discounting is a margin trap. Here is the maths.

Peter Pitcher · 12 August 2026 · 6 min read

On this page The real cost of a discount Why the volume never arrives What to do instead

A £2 discount on a £15 dish feels harmless. Run the numbers and it can demand 40% more volume just to stand still. Most operators never run the numbers.

The real cost of a discount

Discounts come off the margin, not the price. At a 30% margin, £2 off a £15 dish gives away 44% of the profit in that sale. The price cut is visible to the customer once; the margin cut repeats on every plate.

Worked example

  • Dish price £15, margin £4.50
  • With £2 off, margin falls to £2.50
  • Volume needed to stand still on that dish: +80%

A content calendar will not fix a broken growth system. Neither will a discount.

Why the volume never arrives

Discount demand is borrowed, not created. It pulls forward visits from people who were coming anyway, and it anchors new customers to a price you cannot hold. When we tested this at The Anchor, the discounted sessions filled with existing regulars while quiet sessions stayed quiet.

Quick test: if the promotion still looks good with the expected uplift halved, run it. If not, it is a margin leak with a marketing name.

What to do instead

Raise perceived value instead of cutting price. Reprice the anchor items, fix the booking journey, make the busy sessions bookable and the quiet ones worth leaving the house for. Then measure the change against a baseline you agreed before you started.
