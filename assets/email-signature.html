Installing it

Press the copy button, then paste. The button copies the rendered signature rather than the code, which is what every mail client expects. Do not paste the HTML source: Gmail and Outlook will show it as text.

  • Gmail: Settings, See all settings, General, Signature. Create one called "New" and one called "Reply", then set the defaults underneath so new mail uses the full signature and replies use the short one.
  • Outlook (web): Settings, Mail, Compose and reply, Email signature. Same two, with the dropdowns below choosing which goes where.
  • Outlook (desktop): File, Options, Mail, Signatures.
  • Apple Mail: Settings, Signatures. Untick "Always match my default message font", or it will strip the styling.

The logo has to be hosted, not attached. It loads from orangejelly.co.uk/brand/email-signature-logo.png, so it only appears once that file is live on the site. If you would rather not wait for a deploy, Gmail and Outlook will both host it for you: delete the broken image in the signature editor, then use the editor's own insert image control and pick public/brand/email-signature-logo.png off the Mac. Set it to 180 pixels wide either way.

Decisions behind it

  • Palette straight from the site. Ink #23252e, orange #f76b0c for the rules, deep orange #b34e08 for links. Deep orange rather than brand orange for anything that is text: brand orange on white measures 2.97:1, which fails WCAG AA, while deep orange measures 5.24:1 and passes.
  • Logo rebuilt at email weight. The site file is 194KB. A signature rides on every message you send, so it is served at 6.9KB from /brand/email-signature-logo.png, at twice its display size so it stays sharp on a retina screen.
  • Flattened onto white, not transparent. Outlook composites transparent PNGs against black.
  • Tables and inline styles only. Outlook renders mail through Word, which has no flexbox, no grid and no stylesheet.
  • Schibsted Grotesk first, then Helvetica and Arial. Mail clients will not load a webfont. Where the brand face is installed it is used, and elsewhere it falls back to something of the same character.
  • No emoji and no WhatsApp badge. Both belonged to the old hospitality positioning. Emoji also render differently on every platform and read as informal in a first approach to a business owner.
  • The words are the site's own. The descriptor is the footer category line and the closing line is the footer strapline, so the two surfaces cannot drift apart.

The legal line is not decoration. A UK limited company has to give its registered name, company number, place of registration and registered office address on its business email. Those details are taken from the public register: Orange Jelly Limited, 10537179, incorporated 22 December 2016. Worth a glance in case the registered office has moved since the last filing.
