Food rules protect guests and let pubs run more functions safely. Parties test them: one person orders for forty, weeks ahead.

`[HEADLINE]`
# Seven food checks before your next party booking

`[BYLINE SENTENCE, bold]`
Here, Peter Pitcher MBII sets out seven checks, each marked as law or Food Standards Agency (FSA) advice:

`[SECTION 1]`
## 1. Ask every guest
**FSA advice:** ask all customers whether they have any allergen requirements. Put it on the booking form.

`[SECTION 2]`
## 2. Ask again on the day
**FSA advice:** for a group pre-order, discuss allergens with each guest on the day, before food is served.

`[SECTION 3]`
## 3. Know every dish and drink
**The law:** you must be able to tell guests which of the 14 listed allergens are in each one, free food included. If staff tell them in person, a notice must say to ask.

`[SECTION 4]`
## 4. Label the buffet dish by dish
**FSA advice:** give allergen information for each item separately, not for the buffet as a whole.

`[SECTION 5]`
## 5. Recheck the delivery
**FSA advice:** check deliveries are what you ordered. A different product may contain different allergens. Shop-bought sausage rolls too: keep the packaging.

`[SECTION 6]`
## 6. One plate, one name
**FSA advice:** pass the order to the kitchen in writing, make the plate easy to identify and have the server confirm it at the table.

`[SECTION 7]`
## 7. Watch the buffet clock
**The law** in England, Wales and Northern Ireland: cold food at 8°C or below, hot food at 63°C or above. The only exception is one controlled spell outside that: under four hours cold, under two hours hot.

`[LAST LINES]`
Brief everyone on shift: nobody guesses. FSA advice covers England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Scotland's guidance and temperature rules differ. For the full rules: GOV.UK, Food Standards Scotland or your council's food safety team.
